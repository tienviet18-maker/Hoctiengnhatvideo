The five nobles each set out to search for their treasure. ‖ それぞれ: each. 〜に出かける: go out to ~.
However, none of them was something easily found. ‖ 簡単に: easily. 見つかる: be found.
The first, Prince Ishitsukuri, couldn't be bothered to go all the way to faraway India. ‖ 一人目: the first person. 面倒: bothersome.
So he secretly took an old, blackened bowl from a nearby mountain temple. ‖ そこで: so. 山寺: mountain temple. こっそり: secretly.
The prince put it in a splendid box and presented it to Princess Kaguya. ‖ 立派: splendid. 差し出す: present, hold out.
But when Princess Kaguya looked, the bowl wasn't glowing at all. ‖ 少しも〜ない: not at all.
If it were the Buddha's bowl, it should shine. This is just an old bowl. ‖ 〜なら: if it's ~. 〜はずだ: should ~. ただの: just, mere.
His lie exposed, the prince went home in shame. ‖ うそがばれる: a lie is exposed. はずかしい: ashamed.
The second, Prince Kuramochi, was a very cunning man. ‖ ずるい: sly, cunning.
He left the capital saying he was going on a journey, but in fact he was secretly hiding at home. ‖ 旅に出る: set off on a journey. 実は: actually. 隠れる: hide.
Then he gathered skilled craftsmen and made them create an exact replica of the branch in gold and silver. ‖ 腕のいい: skilled. 職人: craftsman. そっくり: exactly alike. 〜させる: make (someone) ~.
Three years later, the prince appeared at the old man's house with the branch. ‖ 〜後: ~ later. 現れる: appear.
I met storms at sea, even encountered ogres, and found Mount Hōrai at the risk of my life. ‖ 嵐にあう: be caught in a storm. 出会う: encounter. 命がけ: risking one's life.
The prince's story sounded just like a real adventure. ‖ まるで〜のようだ: just like ~. 冒険: adventure.
My, what a magnificent branch. Kaguya, there's nothing for it but to marry now. ‖ 見事: magnificent. 〜しかない: have no choice but ~.
Princess Kaguya looked troubled and fell silent. ‖ 困る: be troubled. 黙る: fall silent.
Just then, six men came into the house holding papers. ‖ そのとき: just then. 紙: paper.
Your Highness, we still haven't been paid for making the branch! ‖ 代金: payment, fee. 〜ておりません: have not ~ (humble).
We worked a thousand days to finally make that golden branch! ‖ 千日: a thousand days. やっと: finally.
With that, the prince's lie was completely exposed. ‖ すっかり: completely. ばれる: come out, be exposed.
Oh, I thought it was real and was so worried. I'm glad it's a fake. ‖ 本物: the real thing. 作り物: fake, imitation. よかった: what a relief.
Princess Kaguya sighed with relief and smiled. ‖ ほっとする: be relieved. にっこり: smile.
The third, the Minister of the Right, was enormously rich. ‖ たいへんな: enormous. お金持ち: rich person.
The Minister paid a merchant from Tang China a great deal of gold and bought a robe of fire-rat hide. ‖ 唐: Tang China. 商人: merchant. はらう: pay.
Now Princess Kaguya is mine! ‖ 私のもの: mine.
Princess Kaguya looked at the robe and said: ‖ 着物: robe, kimono.
If it's real fire-rat hide, it shouldn't burn even in a fire. Let's test it. ‖ 〜ても: even if ~. 〜はずだ: should ~. ためす: test.
When they put the robe in the fire, it went up in flames and soon turned to ash. ‖ めらめら: (burning) in flames. 灰: ash.
The Minister turned pale and went home without a word. ‖ 顔を青くする: turn pale. 〜ずに: without ~ing.
The fourth, the Great Counselor, ordered his retainers to go fetch the jewel from the dragon's neck. ‖ 家来: retainer, servant. 取ってこい: go get it (command). 命令する: order.
But the retainers were afraid of the dragon, and none of them would go. ‖ こわい: scared. 〜(よ)うとしない: won't ~.
Enough! I'll go get it myself! ‖ もういい: enough. 自分で: by oneself.
The Great Counselor boarded a ship himself and set out to sea. ‖ 船: ship.
At once a great storm arose, and the ship was tossed about like a leaf. ‖ 起こる: arise. 木の葉: leaf. ゆれる: sway, rock.
Thunder rumbled, and aboard the ship the Great Counselor felt sick again and again. ‖ 雷: thunder. ごろごろ: rumble. 気分が悪い: feel sick.
Dragon god, forgive me! I'll never again dream of taking your jewel! ‖ 許す: forgive. 二度と〜ない: never again. 〜などと: such a thing as ~.
When the storm stopped, the Great Counselor's eyes were red and swollen like plums. ‖ やむ: stop (rain, storm). すもも: plum. はれる: swell.
The Great Counselor gave up on the dragon's jewel and went home. ‖ あきらめる: give up.
The fifth, the Middle Counselor, tried to take the cowrie shell from a swallow's nest. ‖ 巣: nest. 〜(よ)うとする: try to ~.
The Middle Counselor sat in a basket and had himself hoisted up to the nest on a high roof. ‖ つり上げる: hoist up. 〜てもらう: have someone ~.
When he put his hand in the nest, he touched something hard. ‖ 固い: hard. さわる: touch.
Overjoyed, the Middle Counselor fell while still clutching it. ‖ にぎる: grip. 〜たまま: while still ~. 落ちる: fall.
When he opened his hand, it wasn't a cowrie shell but swallow droppings. ‖ 〜ではなく: not ~ but. ふん: droppings.
The Middle Counselor hurt his back badly and was bedridden from then on. ‖ 腰を打つ: hurt one's back. 寝こむ: be laid up in bed.
And so, of the five, not one was able to bring a treasure. ‖ 〜のうち: among ~. 持ってこられる: be able to bring.

If she's such a beautiful princess, I must meet her at least once. ‖ それほど: that much. ぜひ: by all means.

So the Emperor pretended to go hunting and secretly visited the bamboo cutter's house. ‖ 狩り: hunting. 〜ふりをする: pretend to ~. たずねる: visit.
When the Emperor entered the room, a beautiful princess shining with light was sitting there. ‖ かがやく: shine, sparkle.
How beautiful. Won't you please come to court with me? ‖ 〜てくれないか: won't you ~?
I am not of this land. You cannot take me with you. ‖ お連れになる: take along (honorific). 〜ことはできない: cannot ~.

When Princess Kaguya returned to her form, the Emperor went home sadly. ‖ さびしそう: looking lonely.
For three years after that, the Emperor and Princess Kaguya shared their hearts through letters alone. ‖ 心を通わせる: have an understanding of hearts.
But from around that time, whenever Princess Kaguya looked at the moon, she would weep sadly. ‖ 〜ては: every time ~. 〜ようになる: come to ~.
Why is Princess Kaguya crying? The secret will be revealed in the final episode. ‖ 秘密: secret. 明かされる: be revealed.
