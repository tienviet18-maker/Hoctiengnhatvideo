Long, long ago, in a mountain village deep in snow, there lived a poor old man and old woman. ‖ まずしい: poor. 深い: deep.
The two worked hard, but life was never easy for them. ‖ まじめ: hard-working, earnest. 暮らし: living, life. 楽: easy, comfortable.
One New Year's Eve, there was not a single coin in the house to buy rice cakes for the New Year. ‖ 大みそか: New Year's Eve. お正月: New Year. 少しも〜ない: not at all.
Grandma, tomorrow is New Year's Day, and yet we don't have even one rice cake. ‖ 〜というのに: even though. ばあさん: old woman (husband addressing wife).
It's New Year, of all times, and it's so dreary. ‖ せっかく〜のに: it's a special ~, and yet. さびしい: lonely, dreary.
Oh, I know, dear. Why not take the hats I made into town to sell? ‖ 笠: woven hat. 〜てはどうでしょう: how about ~.
During the autumn, the old woman had woven five hats from a grass called suge. ‖ すげ: sedge grass. 編む: weave. 〜ておく: do in advance.
That's a fine idea. I'll sell the hats and bring back some rice cakes. ‖ 〜てこよう: go and ~ (and come back).
The old man carried the five hats on his back and set off along the snowy road to town. ‖ しょう: carry on the back. せなか: back.
The town was bustling with people doing their New Year shopping. ‖ にぎわう: be lively, bustling. 買い物: shopping.
Hats! Who wants a hat? Nice warm hats! ‖ 〜はいらんかね: anyone want ~? (street cry).
The old man called out loudly, but no one stopped. ‖ 呼ぶ: call out. 足を止める: stop walking.
Old man, nobody buys hats on New Year's Eve. ‖ 〜なんて: the likes of ~ (dismissive, surprise).
Everyone's busy buying rice cakes and fish. ‖ いそがしい: busy.
I see. I suppose that's true. ‖ それもそうだ: that's true too.
Even as the sun sank low, not a single hat was sold. ‖ 日がかたむく: the sun sinks. 売れる: sell, be sold.
It can't be helped. There's nothing to do but go home today. ‖ しかたがない: it can't be helped. 〜しかない: have no choice but ~.
Dragging his heavy feet, the old man started back toward the village. ‖ ひきずる: drag. 〜はじめる: begin ~.
Before long, snow began falling heavily from the sky. ‖ そのうちに: before long. どんどん: more and more.
The wind grew strong too, so much that he could hardly see ahead. ‖ 〜ほど: so much that ~.
When he reached the field at the village entrance, six Jizō statues stood in a row by the road. ‖ お地蔵さま: Jizō (guardian Buddhist statue). 道ばた: roadside. 野原: field.
Snow was piled high on the Jizō statues' heads and shoulders. ‖ 肩: shoulder. 積もる: pile up.
Oh, dear Jizō. In this snow, you must be so cold. ‖ さぞ〜でしょう: surely must be ~.
The old man carefully brushed the snow off each statue's head, one by one. ‖ ていねい: careful. はらう: brush off.
I know. Please wear these hats. ‖ 〜ていただく: humbly have (someone) ~.
One by one, the old man put the unsold hats on the Jizō statues. ‖ かぶせる: put on (someone's head). 〜ずつ: one at a time.
But there were only five hats. There wasn't one for the sixth Jizō. ‖ 〜しか〜ない: only ~. 分: share, portion. 足りない: not enough.
Oh dear. This way, one of them will stay cold. ‖ 〜まま: remaining as is.
The old man took the hand towel off his own head and put it on the sixth Jizō. ‖ 手ぬぐい: tenugui, cloth hand towel. はずす: take off.
I'm sorry it's just an old towel. Please make do with this. ‖ 申しわけありません: I'm terribly sorry. がまん: putting up with.
Then he put his hands together in prayer and walked home through the snow. ‖ 手を合わせる: press palms together.
Welcome home. Goodness, your head is all white with snow. ‖ 真っ白: pure white.
I'm sorry, Grandma. I couldn't sell a single hat. ‖ すまない: sorry (casual).
The old man told her how he had put the hats on the Jizō statues. ‖ 〜てくる: do ~ (before coming back).
Instead of rice cakes, I've brought nothing home. ‖ 〜のかわりに: instead of ~.
No, dear. That was a good thing you did. ‖ いいこと: a good deed.
Even without rice cakes, I'm happier knowing the Jizō aren't cold. ‖ 〜ても: even if ~. そのほうが: that way is better.
The two drank some hot water to warm up and decided to go to bed early. ‖ お湯: hot water. あたためる: warm up. 〜ことにする: decide to ~.
Though they thought, "A New Year without rice cakes...", their hearts felt warm all the same, even more so. ‖ 〜ながらも: although ~. かえって: on the contrary, rather.
Late that night, they heard the crunch, crunch of footsteps in the snow outside. ‖ ざくざく: crunching. ふむ: step on.
Where is the old man's house? Where is the kind old man who gave us hats? ‖ やさしい: kind.
The voices came closer and closer and stopped in front of the house. ‖ だんだん: gradually. 近づく: approach.
There was a thud, as if something heavy had been set down. ‖ どすん: thud. 置く: put down.
Dear, what was that sound just now? ‖ 今の: just now.
Who knows. Who could it be in the middle of the night? ‖ さあ: well, who knows. 夜中: middle of the night.
When the two quietly opened the door, they found big straw bales piled like a mountain in front of the house. ‖ そっと: quietly. 俵: straw bale. 〜てある: has been ~ (state).
There were bales of rice, rice cakes, fish, vegetables, and even a box full of gold koban coins. ‖ 小判: koban gold coin. 〜まで: even ~.
My, my. What on earth is this? ‖ いったい: what on earth.
Looking into the distance, they saw the Jizō in their hats walking away through the snow. ‖ 〜ところだ: be just in the middle of ~.
The last Jizō of all was wearing that old hand towel. ‖ いちばん後ろ: the very back.
The Jizō came to thank us. ‖ お礼: thanks. 〜てくださる: (someone) kindly ~ for us.
The old man and woman knelt in the snow and bowed deeply. ‖ 頭を下げる: bow.
Thank you. Thank you so much. ‖ ありがとうございます: thank you.
The next morning, the two grilled rice cakes and welcomed a very happy New Year. ‖ 焼く: grill. むかえる: welcome.
And they shared rice and rice cakes with the poor people of the village, too. ‖ 分ける: share. 〜てあげる: do ~ for others.
After that, every time the old man passed the Jizō, he put his hands together. ‖ 〜たびに: every time ~.
When it snowed, he brushed the snow off their heads, and in summer he offered them cool water. ‖ 〜ば: when ~. そなえる: make an offering.
Even today, when winter comes, the story of Kasa Jizō is read by children in Japan. ‖ 〜と: whenever ~. 読まれる: be read.
Being kind to others even when you are poor yourself: perhaps that heart is the greatest treasure of all. ‖ 〜こそ: precisely ~. 〜かもしれない: perhaps.
