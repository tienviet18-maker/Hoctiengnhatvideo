Long ago, in a certain village, there lived an old man and an old woman. ‖ 住む: live. ある〜: a certain ~.
The two had no children, and that was the loneliest thing of all for them. ‖ 何よりも: more than anything. さびしい: lonely.
One day, the two went to pray to the god of Sumiyoshi. ‖ お参り: visiting a shrine to pray. 神様: god.
Please, bless us with a child. No matter how small, we don't mind. ‖ 授ける: grant, bestow. どんなに〜でも: no matter how ~. かまいません: it's fine.
May a child be born to us. ‖ 〜ますように: may ~ (a wish).
And then, some time later, a baby really was born to the old woman. ‖ しばらくして: a while later. 本当に: really.
But the baby was no bigger than the old man's finger. ‖ 指: finger. 〜ほど: about as ~. 〜しか〜ない: only.
What a tiny child! But he is a precious child, a gift from the god. ‖ なんと〜だろう: how ~! いただく: receive (humble).
The two raised him with great love. ‖ かわいがる: dote on. 育てる: raise.
However, no matter how many years passed, the child didn't grow even a little. ‖ 何年たっても: however many years pass. ちっとも〜ない: not at all.
He stayed just one sun tall, about three centimeters in today's terms. ‖ 背の高さ: height. 一寸: one sun (old unit, ~3 cm). 〜のまま: remain ~.
So the villagers came to call him Issun-bōshi, "the one-sun boy." ‖ 呼ぶ: call. 〜ようになる: come to ~.
Hey, shorty! Little bean! ‖ ちび: shorty (teasing). 豆つぶ: a bean.
Even when the village children laughed at him, Issun-bōshi was always smiling. ‖ 笑われる: be laughed at. にこにこ: smiling.
My body may be small, but my heart is big. ‖ 〜ても: even if ~. 心: heart.
Issun-bōshi was a clever and very lively child. ‖ 頭がいい: smart. 元気: lively, healthy.
One spring day, Issun-bōshi sat down properly in front of the old man and woman. ‖ きちんと: properly. 座る: sit.
Grandpa, Grandma. I have a favor to ask. ‖ お願い: request.
I want to go to the capital and become a fine samurai. ‖ 都: the capital. りっぱ: fine, splendid. 侍: samurai.
Oh my, to the capital? With such a small body, will you be all right? ‖ そんな: such. 大丈夫かい: are you OK? (casual).
I'll be fine. I'll surely become someone great and come back. ‖ 必ず: surely. 帰ってくる: come back.
I see. If you feel that strongly, go on then. ‖ そこまで: that far. 行っておいで: off you go.
The old woman gave him a sewing needle to use instead of a sword. ‖ 針: needle. 〜のかわりに: instead of ~. 渡す: hand over.
Use this as your sword. For the sheath, I'll make one from this piece of wheat straw. ‖ さや: sheath. 麦わら: wheat straw. 〜てあげる: do for someone.
For a boat, use this wooden bowl. For an oar, one chopstick is all you need. ‖ おわん: wooden bowl. かい: oar. 〜ばいい: ~ is enough.
Issun-bōshi tucked the needle sword at his waist and climbed into his bowl boat. ‖ 腰にさす: wear at the waist. 乗る: get on/in.
I'm off! I'll surely become a fine samurai! ‖ 行ってきます: I'm off. きっと: surely.
The bowl boat rode the river's current and drifted on and on downstream. ‖ 流れに乗る: ride the current. どんどん: steadily. 下る: go down(stream).
On rainy days, he used a leaf as a roof inside the bowl. ‖ 葉っぱ: leaf. 屋根: roof.
Whenever fish came close, he drove them away with his needle sword. ‖ 近づく: approach. 追いはらう: drive away.
I won't give up. I'm going to the capital. ‖ 負ける: lose. 〜ぞ: (emphatic, male).
After many days of travel, Issun-bōshi finally reached the capital. ‖ 旅をする: travel. とうとう: at last. 着く: arrive.
The capital was bustling, full of people, horses and carts. ‖ 馬: horse. 車: cart (ox cart). にぎわい: bustle.
Wow, so many people. I'd better be careful not to get stepped on. ‖ 踏まれる: be stepped on. 〜ないように: so as not to ~. 〜なくちゃ: must ~.
Issun-bōshi found the biggest mansion in town. It belonged to a powerful minister. ‖ 屋敷: mansion. えらい: important, high-ranking. 大臣: minister.
Issun-bōshi stood beside the wooden clogs at the entrance and called out in a loud voice. ‖ げんかん: entrance. 下駄: wooden clogs. 横: beside.
Hello! Is anyone home? Hello! ‖ ごめんください: greeting when visiting a house.
Hm? I hear a voice, but there's no one here. ‖ はて: hmm (puzzled). 声がする: a voice is heard.
Here, down here! Beside the clogs! ‖ そば: right next to.
When the minister looked down, he saw a tiny, tiny boy hopping up and down. ‖ ぴょんぴょん: hop hop. 跳びはねる: jump about.
My word, to think there is such a tiny person! What have you come here for? ‖ 〜とは: (surprise) to think ~. 〜をしに来る: come to do ~.
My name is Issun-bōshi. I have come to the capital to become a samurai. Please let me work here. ‖ 申す: be called (humble). 〜ために: in order to ~. 働かせてください: please let me work.
Oh ho, how interesting. Then show me what you can do. ‖ ほう: oh ho. 見せてごらん: show me.
Standing on the minister's palm, Issun-bōshi showed off his sword drills with the needle: "Hai! Yah!" ‖ 手のひら: palm. けいこ: practice, training.
So small, yet quite impressive. Very well, I'll have you work here. ‖ 〜のに: even though. 見事: splendid. 〜てもらう: have someone do ~.
And so it was decided that Issun-bōshi would work in the minister's mansion. ‖ こうして: in this way. 〜ことになる: it is decided that ~.
The minister had a beautiful and kind daughter, a princess. ‖ 美しい: beautiful. 優しい: kind. お姫様: princess.
Oh, what an adorable little samurai. I look forward to your service. ‖ かわいらしい: adorable. よろしくね: nice to have you (casual).
Issun-bōshi attended the princess and worked as hard as he could every day. ‖ お供をする: attend, accompany. いっしょうけんめい: with all one's might.
When the princess read a book, he helped her turn the pages. ‖ めくる: turn (a page). 手伝う: help.
When the princess wrote a letter, he helped by grinding ink on the inkstone. ‖ 硯: inkstone. 墨をする: grind ink.
Issun-bōshi may be small, but he notices things better than anyone. ‖ 気がつく: be attentive. 〜けれど: although.
The people of the mansion, too, gradually came to treasure Issun-bōshi. ‖ だんだん: gradually. 大事にする: treasure.
Grandpa, Grandma. I'm doing well here in the capital. ‖ 元気にやる: get along well.
Each night, Issun-bōshi murmured this while gazing at the sky toward his faraway village. ‖ 〜ながら: while ~. つぶやく: murmur.
But Issun-bōshi did not know yet. ‖ まだ〜ない: not yet.
That ahead of him, a battle with terrifying ogres was waiting. ‖ この先: from now on, ahead. 恐ろしい: terrifying. 鬼: ogre, oni. 戦い: battle.
Can little Issun-bōshi beat the ogres? Find out in the next episode. ‖ 勝てる: can win. 続き: continuation.
