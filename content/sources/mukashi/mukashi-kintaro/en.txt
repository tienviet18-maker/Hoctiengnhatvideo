Long, long ago, on Mount Ashigara in the province of Sagami, a boy named Kintarō lived with his mother. ‖ 〜という: named ~. 男の子: boy.
Kintarō wore a red bib and always carried a big axe on his shoulder. ‖ 腹掛け: bib-apron. まさかり: broad axe. かつぐ: carry on the shoulder.
Even as a small child, Kintarō was so strong that not even grown-ups could match him. ‖ かなわない: can't match. 〜ほど: so ~ that.
Kintarō, don't go too far, all right? ‖ 〜てはいけません: you must not ~.
It's fine, Mother. I'm just going to play with the mountain animals. ‖ 〜だけ: just.
Kintarō's friends were the rabbit, the monkey, the deer and the bear. ‖ うさぎ: rabbit. さる: monkey. しか: deer. くま: bear.
Every day they all ran about and climbed trees, playing in the mountains. ‖ 〜たり〜たり: doing things like ~ and ~. 登る: climb.
Kintarō, let's have a sumo match today. ‖ 相撲をとる: wrestle sumo.
All right, come at me, anyone you like! ‖ かかってこい: come at me.
The animals charged at Kintarō one after another, but none of them could win. ‖ 次々に: one after another. 向かう: go at. 勝つ: win.
Kintarō, the more you wrestle, the stronger you get! ‖ 〜ば〜ほど: the more ~, the more ~.
Just then, the biggest bear on the mountain came lumbering over. ‖ のっしのっし: lumbering heavily.
Fight me. I'm the strongest on this mountain. ‖ 勝負: contest. おれ: I (rough, male).
Sure. But don't cry if you lose. ‖ 負ける: lose. 〜なよ: don't ~.
Kintarō and the bear locked together in a tight grip. ‖ がっぷり: firmly. 組み合う: grapple.
The bear growled and pushed, but Kintarō didn't budge an inch. ‖ うなる: growl. 押す: push. びくともしない: not budge.
With a shout of "Hah!", Kintarō threw the huge bear. ‖ 声をあげる: shout. 投げ飛ばす: throw.
I give up. I'm no match for you at all. ‖ まいった: I give up. とても〜ない: not at all.
You were pretty strong too. Let's be friends from now on. ‖ なかなか: quite.
From then on, the bear would carry Kintarō on his back around the mountain. ‖ それからというもの: from then on. 背中: back. 〜ようになる: come to ~.
For Kintarō, the mountain animals were precious companions, like family. ‖ 〜にとって: for ~, to ~. 仲間: companion.
One day, they all set off beyond the mountain to gather chestnuts. ‖ 栗拾い: chestnut gathering.
But after heavy rain, the river had overflowed and the bridge had been washed away. ‖ あふれる: overflow. 流される: be washed away.
Now we can't get to the other bank. ‖ 向こう岸: other bank. 渡る: cross.
If we go the long way round, it'll get dark. ‖ 遠回り: detour. 日が暮れる: get dark.
What a problem. But I can't just go home now. ‖ 困る: be in trouble. 〜わけにはいかない: can't (reasonably) ~.
Kintarō looked up at a big cedar tree standing on the riverbank. ‖ 川岸: riverbank. 杉: cedar. 見上げる: look up at.
All right, let's make this tree a bridge. ‖ 〜にしよう: let's make it ~.
Kintarō wrapped both arms around the tree and pushed hard. ‖ 抱える: hold in one's arms. ぐいぐい: forcefully.
With a cracking, creaking sound, the great cedar fell across to the other bank. ‖ めりめり: creaking crack. 倒れる: fall.
Amazing! It's a wonderful bridge! ‖ 立派: splendid.
The overjoyed animals crossed the wooden bridge. ‖ 大喜び: great joy.
Some samurai passing along the mountain path were watching the whole scene closely. ‖ 様子: scene. 侍: samurai. じっと: intently.
My lord, look at that child. He knocked down that big cedar all by himself. ‖ 殿: my lord. ご覧ください: please look. 〜ぞ: (emphatic, archaic).
What strength! I'd like to meet that boy. ‖ なんという: what a ~. 〜てみたい: want to try ~.
The leader of those samurai was Minamoto no Raikō, a famous warrior of the capital. ‖ 大将: leader. 武士: warrior.
Raikō visited Kintarō's home and met his mother. ‖ たずねる: visit.
I would like to take your boy to the capital as my retainer. I intend to raise him into a fine samurai. ‖ 家来: retainer. 〜つもり: intend to ~.
Oh, what a gracious offer. ‖ ありがたい: grateful, gracious.
His mother was happy, but on the other hand she was terribly sad at parting with Kintarō. ‖ 〜反面: on the other hand. 〜てたまらない: unbearably ~.
Kintarō, what do you want to do? ‖ どうしたい: what do you want to do.
I want to become a strong samurai and help people in trouble. ‖ 困っている人: people in trouble.
But I'm worried about leaving you all alone, Mother. ‖ 一人にする: leave alone.
Don't worry. Seeing you grow into a fine man is my greatest wish. ‖ 願い: wish.
It's precisely because I love you that I want to send you off. ‖ 〜からこそ: precisely because ~. 送り出す: send off.
Holding back his tears, Kintarō nodded. ‖ 涙をこらえる: hold back tears. うなずく: nod.
On the morning he left, all the mountain animals came to see him off. ‖ 出発: departure. 見送り: seeing off.
Kintarō, don't forget us, even in the capital. ‖ 忘れる: forget.
As if I'd forget! Everyone, please take care of my mother for me. ‖ 〜もんか: as if I would ~. よろしく頼む: please look after.
Leave it to us. We'll look after her properly. ‖ まかせる: leave to. ちゃんと: properly.
Kintarō went down Mount Ashigara, looking back again and again. ‖ ふり返る: look back.
On reaching the capital, Kintarō changed his name to Sakata no Kintoki. ‖ あらためる: change.
Every day, Kintoki worked hard at sword and archery practice. ‖ 剣: sword. 弓: bow. けいこにはげむ: train hard.
And he became such a samurai that he was counted among Raikō's four retainers, the "Four Heavenly Kings". ‖ 四天王: Four Heavenly Kings. 数えられる: be counted.
It is said that with his great strength, Kintoki protected the people of the capital from villains many times. ‖ 悪者: villain. 〜ということです: it is said.
Even today in Japan, some homes display Kintarō dolls on Children's Day. ‖ 子どもの日: Children's Day (May 5). 人形: doll. かざる: display.
They carry the wish that children will grow up strong and kind. ‖ 〜ように: so that ~. こめる: put into.
Kintarō of Mount Ashigara: the tale of a lively boy loved by everyone, then and now. ‖ 愛される: be loved. 元気: lively.
