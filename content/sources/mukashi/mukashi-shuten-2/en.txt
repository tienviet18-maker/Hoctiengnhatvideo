Inside the gate stood a great mansion adorned with gold and silver. ‖ 金: gold. 銀: silver. かざる: decorate. 屋敷: mansion.
In a wide hall, dozens of oni had gathered and were drinking noisily. ‖ 何十匹: dozens. にぎやか: lively.
The one seated at the very back was Shuten-dōji. ‖ 一番奥: the very back.
His face was smooth like a child's, but his body was huge as a mountain and his eyes glowed red. ‖ つるり: smooth. 〜のように: like ~.
Oho, human guests, how rare. You yamabushi, where have you come from? ‖ 〜とは: (surprise) ~ of all things. 客: guest.
We train in the mountains of Dewa. We lost our way and ended up here. ‖ 修行: training. 迷う: get lost. 〜てしまう: end up ~.
I see, I see. Tonight there's a feast. Don't hold back, drink with us. ‖ 遠慮せずに: without holding back. 〜ていけ: do ~ before you go.
The dishes the oni served were all strange things they had never seen. ‖ 見たこともない: never seen. あやしい: suspicious, strange. 〜ばかり: nothing but.
Kintoki grimaced, but Raikō smiled and ate it all to show them. ‖ 顔をしかめる: grimace. 〜てみせる: do ~ to show.
Ha ha ha! Quite a brave monk, aren't you? I like you. ‖ 度胸: nerve, courage. 坊主: monk. 気に入る: take a liking.
Drunk on sake, Shuten-dōji began telling his own story. ‖ 酔う: get drunk. 昔話: old story.
Long ago I lived on Mount Hiei. But a great monk drove me off the mountain. ‖ えらい: great, eminent. 追い出す: drive out.
After that I wandered from mountain to mountain, and at last I reached this Mount Ōe. ‖ さまよう: wander. やっと: at last. たどり着く: finally reach.
Humans call us oni and fear us. But I have never told a single lie. ‖ おそれる: fear. 〜たことがない: have never ~.
Raikō nodded quietly as he listened to the story. ‖ うなずく: nod. 〜ながら: while ~.
In thanks for tonight, won't you please drink the sake we brought? ‖ お礼に: as thanks. 飲んでいただけませんか: would you kindly drink.
It is a special sake offered to the mountain gods. ‖ ささげる: offer. 特別: special.
Special sake, you say? Then I must drink it. ‖ 〜だと: ~, you say? 〜たいものだ: really want to ~.
Raikō poured that Jinben Kidoku sake into Shuten-dōji's great cup. ‖ さかずき: sake cup. 注ぐ: pour.
Delicious! I've never had sake this good in my life. Pour more! ‖ 生まれて初めて: for the first time in one's life.
His underlings drank the sake too, one after another. ‖ 手下: underling. 次々に: one after another.
Before long, the oni toppled over, one, then another, then another. ‖ しばらくすると: after a while. ばたばた: (fall) one after another. 倒れる: fall over.
Somehow I've suddenly gotten sleepy. I'll go rest in the back. ‖ なんだか: somehow. 〜てくる: start to ~. 〜とする: decide to ~.
Shuten-dōji staggered into the back room. ‖ ふらふら: staggering.
When the mansion fell quiet, Raikō and his men took their swords out of their back-chests. ‖ 取り出す: take out.
Raikō put on the star helmet the gods had given him, fastening it tight. ‖ もらう: receive. かぶる: put on (headwear).
Everyone, this way. I'll lead you to the oni's bedchamber. ‖ 寝床: bed. ご案内します: I'll guide you (humble).
The woman they had met at the river quietly opened the door and led the group inside. ‖ そっと: quietly. 案内する: guide.
In the room, Shuten-dōji was asleep, snoring loudly. ‖ いびきをかく: snore.
Then, from somewhere, the three old men appeared. ‖ すると: thereupon. 現れる: appear.
Now is the time. We will hold down the oni's arms and legs. ‖ 押さえる: hold down. 〜ておく: do ~ in advance.
The old men bound the oni's hands and feet tightly with thick chains. ‖ 鎖: chain. しばりつける: tie down.
Shuten-dōji, prepare yourself. Learn the suffering of the people of the capital! ‖ 覚悟しろ: prepare yourself. 思い知る: learn the hard way.
As Raikō brought down his sword, Shuten-dōji's eyes flew open. ‖ 振り下ろす: swing down. かっと: (eyes) wide open.
You tricked me! Oni never lie, and yet humans do? ‖ だます: deceive. 〜というのに: and yet.
The oni's head flew high into the air and tried to bite Raikō's head. ‖ 飛び上がる: fly up. かみつく: bite into. 〜(よ)うとする: try to ~.
But the star helmet deflected the oni's fangs. ‖ はね返す: bounce back.
The head fell to the floor and never moved again. ‖ それきり: from then on, no more.
The underlings woke, roared, and charged at them. ‖ 目を覚ます: wake up. おそいかかる: attack.
Come on! This time it's my turn! ‖ 今度こそ: this time for sure. 出番: one's turn.
Kintoki lifted two big oni at once and hurled them into the garden. ‖ まとめて: together. 持ち上げる: lift. 投げ飛ばす: hurl.
Kintoki, behind you! Watch out! ‖ 気をつける: be careful.
Tsuna and the other Heavenly Kings fought together, and at last they defeated the oni. ‖ 力を合わせる: join forces. とうとう: at last.
When the battle was over, Raikō and his men opened the door of the prison deep inside the castle. ‖ 牢: prison, cell.
Inside, the young women taken from the capital were huddled together, trembling. ‖ 身を寄せ合う: huddle together. ふるえる: tremble.
You're safe now. Everyone, let's go back to the capital. ‖ 大丈夫: all right. 〜ましょう: let's ~.
Thank you. We thought we would never go home again. ‖ 二度と〜ない: never again. 〜と思っていた: had thought.
Among them was the counselor's daughter. ‖ 姿: figure.
The group led the rescued women down the mountain. ‖ 連れる: take along. 下りる: descend.
When they returned to the capital, people came out into the streets and welcomed Raikō and his men with applause. ‖ 拍手: applause. 迎える: welcome.
My daughter, thank heaven you're safe. Lord Raikō, I will never forget this kindness as long as I live. ‖ よくぞ: well done, thank heaven. 無事: safe. 一生: lifetime.
The counselor held his daughter tight and shed tears of joy. ‖ うれし涙: tears of joy. 流す: shed.
The Emperor was overjoyed too and gave many rewards to Raikō and the Four Heavenly Kings. ‖ ほうび: reward. 与える: give, bestow.
And so, quiet nights finally returned to the capital. ‖ こうして: and so. ようやく: finally. もどる: return.
Yet Shuten-dōji's last words still linger in many people's hearts today. ‖ けれども: however. 残る: remain.
"Oni never lie." Who was it, really, that told the lie? ‖ どちら: which one. 〜のでしょうか: I wonder ~.
Is it all right to lie if it's for a just cause? What do you think? ‖ 〜のためなら: if it's for ~. 〜てもいい: may ~.
And that is the end of the tale of Shuten-dōji. ‖ おしまい: the end.
