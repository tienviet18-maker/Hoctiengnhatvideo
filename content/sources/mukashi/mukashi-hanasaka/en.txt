Long, long ago, in a certain village, there lived an honest old man and old woman. ‖ 正直: honest.
Next door lived a greedy, mean old man. ‖ 欲ばり: greedy. いじわる: mean.
One day, by the river, the honest old man picked up a hungry little white puppy. ‖ おなかをすかせる: be hungry. 子犬: puppy. 拾う: pick up.
Oh, you must have been cold. Come home with me. ‖ 〜だろう: must have ~. おいで: come.
The couple named the puppy "Shiro" and loved him like their own child. ‖ 名前をつける: name. かわいがる: dote on.
Shiro grew up big and strong, and every day he followed the old man to the field. ‖ すくすく: healthily, quickly. 〜について: following ~.
One day in the field, Shiro suddenly began to bark, scratching at the ground. ‖ 地面: ground. かく: scratch. ほえる: bark.
Dig here, woof woof. Dig here, woof woof. ‖ 掘る: dig. ワンワン: woof.
Oh? You want me to dig here? ‖ 〜と言うのかい: are you saying ~?
When the old man dug with his hoe, gold coins large and small came pouring out. ‖ くわ: hoe. 大判小判: old gold coins. ざくざく: in heaps.
My goodness. Thanks to Shiro, we've become rich. ‖ 〜のおかげで: thanks to ~. お金持ち: rich person.
The old man next door had been peeking at it all from the other side of the hedge. ‖ 垣根: hedge. のぞく: peek.
Hey, lend me that Shiro for a bit. ‖ 貸す: lend.
The neighbor dragged Shiro away by force, though the dog didn't want to go. ‖ いやがる: be unwilling. 無理やり: by force. 引っぱる: drag.
Now, quick, show me where the treasure is. ‖ 宝: treasure. ありか: whereabouts.
Shiro absolutely refused to walk. ‖ どうしても〜ない: absolutely won't ~.
When the man dug by force anyway, all that came up was broken pottery and rubbish. ‖ 無理に: by force. 瀬戸物: pottery. 〜ばかり: nothing but ~.
You stupid dog! Because of you, I've had a terrible time! ‖ 〜め: you ~ (insult). 〜のせいで: because of ~ (blame). ひどい目にあう: have a terrible experience.
The neighbor flew into a rage and struck Shiro hard. ‖ かっとなる: lose one's temper. たたく: hit.
Poor Shiro lay still and never moved again. ‖ そのまま: just like that. 動かなくなる: stop moving.
Shiro, Shiro. It's all because I lent you to him. ‖ 〜ばかりに: just because ~.
Weeping, the honest couple buried Shiro in a corner of the field and planted a little pine tree. ‖ うめる: bury. 松: pine. 植える: plant.
Then, wondrously, the pine tree grew bigger before their very eyes. ‖ みるみるうちに: before one's eyes.
Before many days had passed, it had become a great tree reaching the sky. ‖ 〜ないうちに: before ~. 〜ほど: so much that ~. 大木: huge tree.
That night, Shiro appeared in the old man's dream. ‖ 夢: dream.
Grandpa, make a mortar from that pine tree. And pound rice cakes in it. ‖ 臼: mortar. もちをつく: pound mochi.
The old man did just as he was told, cutting down the pine and making a mortar. ‖ 〜とおりに: just as ~.
When the two of them pounded mochi, thump, thump, gold coins overflowed from the mortar. ‖ ぺったん: thump of pounding. あふれ出る: overflow.
Oh, Shiro has helped us again. ‖ 助ける: help.
Seeing this, the neighbor came over again. ‖ やって来る: come along.
Lend me that mortar for a bit. ‖ 貸す: lend.
But when they pounded mochi at the neighbor's house, nothing came out but dirty mud. ‖ 汚い: dirty. どろ: mud.
A mortar like this can't possibly be any use! ‖ 役に立つ: be useful. 〜わけがない: there's no way ~.
In a rage, the neighbor split the mortar with an axe and burned it to ashes. ‖ おの: axe. 割る: split. 燃やす: burn. 灰: ash.
I came to ask for my mortar back... ‖ 返してもらう: get back.
That thing? I burned it. If you want the ashes, they're over there. Take them. ‖ 〜てやる: (did it) to it.
Sadly, the old man put the ashes in a basket and took them home. ‖ ざる: basket. 持ち帰る: take home.
On the way back, the wind blew, and a little ash fell on a withered cherry tree. ‖ 枯れる: wither. 桜: cherry tree. かかる: fall on.
And what do you know? The dead tree burst into cherry blossom. ‖ どうでしょう: (look) what happened. ぱっと: all at once.
Why, this is Shiro's ash. Shiro has made the flowers bloom for me. ‖ 〜てくれる: do for me.
The old man took the ashes out onto the town's main street. ‖ 大通り: main street.
Let me make dead trees bloom! Let me make dead trees bloom! ‖ 〜ましょう: let me ~.
Just then, a lord's procession was passing by. ‖ 殿様: lord. 行列: procession. 通りかかる: pass by.
You there, old man. Can you really make dead trees bloom? ‖ 本当に: really.
Yes, sir. Please, see for yourself. ‖ ご覧ください: please look.
The old man climbed a tree and scattered a handful of ash. ‖ 登る: climb. ひとつかみ: handful. まく: scatter.
Then every single dead tree burst into full cherry bloom at once. ‖ 〜という〜: every ~. 一度に: all at once. さきほこる: bloom in full glory.
Splendid! You are the finest flower-blooming old man in all Japan. ‖ 見事: splendid. 〜じゃ: (archaic) is.
The lord was overjoyed and gave the old man many rewards. ‖ 大喜び: great joy. ごほうび: reward.
Hearing of this, the neighbor scraped together the leftover ashes. ‖ 残る: remain. かき集める: scrape together.
I'm the finest flower-blooming old man in all Japan! ‖ 〜こそ: it is ~ who.
Then, in front of the lord passing by again, he climbed a tree and scattered the ash. ‖ また: again.
But not a single flower bloomed, and the ash rode the wind straight into the lord's eyes. ‖ 〜ず: without ~. 風にのる: ride the wind. どっと: in a rush.
Argh, my eyes! What are you doing, you insolent fool! ‖ 無礼者: insolent person.
Seize this man! ‖ つかまえる: catch.
The neighbor was caught by the retainers and severely scolded. ‖ 家来: retainer. 叱る: scold.
Just because I was greedy, this happened... ‖ 〜ばっかりに: (casual) just because ~.
They say that from then on, the neighbor became quite meek. ‖ それからというもの: ever since then. おとなしい: meek.
Every spring, the honest old couple watched the cherry blossoms in the field where Shiro's pine had stood. ‖ 毎年: every year.
Shiro, they bloomed beautifully again this year. ‖ 今年: this year.
Good things surely come to the kind-hearted. That was the tale of the old man who made flowers bloom. ‖ きっと: surely.
