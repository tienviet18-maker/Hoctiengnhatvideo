When the girl shut herself in the room once more, the sound of the loom began again: creak, clack, clack. ‖ ふたたび: once again. こもる: shut oneself in. 〜と: when ~.
But this time, the sound seemed much weaker and slower than before. ‖ 今度: this time. ずっと: much (more). 〜く聞こえる: sound ~.
Grandpa, don't you think she's grown thinner than before? ‖ やせる: get thin. 〜と思いませんか: don't you think ~?
Yes, I'm worried too. But we made a promise. ‖ わし: I (old man). 〜からなあ: because ~, you know.
The two of them waited for many days, staring at the door of the room. ‖ 見つめる: stare at. 〜ながら: while ~. 何日も: for many days.
On the morning of the seventh day, the girl came out of the room, unsteady on her feet. ‖ 七日目: the seventh day. ふらふら: unsteadily.
Grandpa, Grandma. It's done again. ‖ できる: be finished.
The second cloth shone even more beautifully than the first. ‖ 二枚目: the second (sheet). 〜よりも: than ~. さらに: even more.
The kimono shop owner showed the cloth to the lord of the castle. ‖ お城: castle. 見せる: show.
The lord was greatly pleased and gave many gold koban. ‖ たいそう: greatly (old-fashioned). くださる: give (honorific).
But the story didn't end there. ‖ ところが: however. 終わる: end.
The lord says he wants one more cloth just like it. ‖ 同じ: same. おっしゃる: say (honorific).
They say this time he'll give as many as a hundred koban. ‖ 〜も: as many as ~. 〜そうです: they say.
A hundred… But her body may not hold out any longer. ‖ しかし: but. もつ: last, hold out. 〜かもしれない: may ~.
Just as the old man was about to refuse, the girl came out from the back. ‖ 〜(よ)うとすると: just as one tries to ~. 断る: refuse.
I'm all right. To thank you for taking care of me, please let me weave just one last piece. ‖ お世話になる: be cared for. お礼に: as thanks. 最後に: lastly.
But please, at least keep your promise. ‖ どうか: please. 〜だけは: at least ~.
The girl smiled sadly and went into the room again. ‖ さびしそうに: sadly. ほほえむ: smile.
The third time, the sound of the loom went on into the night, stopping and starting. ‖ 三度目: the third time. とぎれとぎれ: on and off.
Now and then, they could even hear her labored breathing. ‖ ときどき: sometimes. 〜まで: even ~.
How on earth is she weaving such beautiful cloth? ‖ いったい: on earth. どうやって: how.
And we've hardly bought any thread… ‖ 糸: thread. ほとんど〜ない: hardly any. 〜のに: even though.
Now, now, old woman. We mustn't peek. ‖ これ: now (warning). ばあさん: old woman (husband to wife).
But the old woman was so worried about the girl that she just couldn't hold back. ‖ 〜で: because of ~. どうしても〜ない: just cannot ~. 我慢: patience.
That night, once the old man had fallen asleep, the old woman crept up to the back room. ‖ そっと: quietly. 近づく: approach.
Just a tiny peek… ‖ ほんの少し: just a little. 〜なら: if it's ~.
The old woman peeked inside through the gap in the door. ‖ すき間: gap. 〜てしまう: end up ~.
The one inside was not the girl, but a white crane. ‖ 〜ではなく: not ~ but.
With its long beak, the crane was plucking out its own feathers one by one and weaving them into the loom. ‖ くちばし: beak. 〜ずつ: one ~ at a time. 抜く: pull out. 織りこむ: weave in.
The crane's body had almost no feathers left, and it had grown terribly thin. ‖ ほとんどなくなる: be almost gone. やせ細る: become thin.
Ah…! ‖ (a cry of shock)
At the old woman's voice, the sound of the loom stopped dead. ‖ ぴたりと: abruptly. 止まる: stop.
The next morning, the girl sat before the two of them, holding the finished cloth. ‖ 織り上がる: be woven. 〜の前に: in front of ~.
So you have seen me. ‖ 見てしまわれた: (honorific) you have seen.
I'm sorry… I was so worried about you, I just… ‖ ごめんよ: sorry. つい: without meaning to.
I am the crane that Grandpa saved on that snowy day. ‖ 〜ていただく: receive the favor of ~ (humble).
Wanting to repay your kindness, I took human form and came here. ‖ ご恩を返す: repay a kindness. 姿: form, figure.
But once my true form has been seen, I can no longer stay here. ‖ 見られる: be seen. 〜たら: once ~. いられない: cannot stay.
No… Please don't go. You're our precious daughter, aren't you? ‖ 〜ないでおくれ: please don't ~ (elderly). わしら: we (old man).
Thank you. The days I lived with you both were truly happy. ‖ 日々: days. 幸せ: happy.
This cloth is my last gift to you. Please stay well, forever. ‖ 贈り物: gift. いつまでも: forever.
As she said this, the girl's form quietly changed into a crane. ‖ そう言うと: saying so. すうっと: smoothly.
The crane spread its thin wings and went outside. ‖ やせた: thin. 出ていく: go out.
The winter sky was unbelievably clear. ‖ うそのように: unbelievably. 晴れる: clear up.
Wait! ‖ 〜ておくれ: please ~ (elderly).
The crane circled three times above the house, just like that day he saved it in the rice field. ‖ 〜と同じように: just like ~.
Then, with a sad cry of "Kō", it vanished beyond the mountains. ‖ 悲しそうに: sadly. 消える: disappear.

The old woman sank down right where she was and wept. ‖ その場: on the spot. 座りこむ: sink down.
The old man gently laid his hand on her shoulder. ‖ 肩: shoulder. 置く: place.
It's all right. She came to us because she cared for us. ‖ 〜のことを思う: care about ~. 〜てくれる: do ~ for us.
We will never forget her, as long as we live. ‖ 一生: all one's life.
From then on, every time winter came, the two of them looked up at the sky. ‖ 〜たびに: every time ~. 見上げる: look up.
They say that whenever a white crane flew by, they waved and called out, "Are you well?" ‖ 手をふる: wave. 声をかける: call out. 〜そうです: they say.
This story carries down to us the beauty of repaying kindness and the importance of keeping promises. ‖ 恩を返す: repay a kindness. 伝える: pass down. 今に: to the present.
And you, are you keeping your promises to the people who matter to you? ‖ 守れる: be able to keep.
And that is the end of the story of the crane's gift. ‖ おしまい: the end.
