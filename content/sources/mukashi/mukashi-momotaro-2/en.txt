The next morning, Momotarō and his friends found a small boat on the beach. ‖ 浜辺: beach. 船: boat. 見つける: to find.
An old fisherman had lent it to them, saying, "If you're going to Onigashima, use this boat." ‖ 漁師: fisherman. 〜なら: if (that's the case). 貸す: to lend.
The monkey rowed with the oar, the dog kept watch ahead, and the pheasant showed the way from the sky. ‖ かいをこぐ: to row with an oar. 見張る: to keep watch.
Gradually the sea grew rough. Tall waves rocked the boat left and right. ‖ 荒れる: (sea) to get rough. 波: wave. 左右: left and right. ゆらす: to rock.
Whoa, scary! I've never seen waves like these. ‖ こわい: scary. 初めて: for the first time.
Hang in there, monkey. I'm right here with you. ‖ しっかり: firmly, hold on. ついている: to be by someone's side.
The two who had been quarreling until yesterday were now helping each other. ‖ 昨日まで: until yesterday. 助け合う: to help one another.
That's the spirit. If we join forces, no wave can scare us. ‖ その調子: that's the way. 〜ば: if ~.
Before long, under the black clouds, the great rocks of Onigashima came into view. ‖ やがて: before long. 岩: rock. 見えてくる: to come into view.
On the island stood a huge iron gate, shut tight. ‖ 鉄: iron. 門: gate. かたく: tightly.
I'll fly ahead and see what's going on inside. ‖ 先に: ahead. 様子: situation.
The pheasant soared high and flew over to the other side of the gate. ‖ 舞い上がる: to soar up. 向こう: the other side.
After a while, the pheasant came back and perched on Momotarō's shoulder. ‖ もどる: to return. 肩: shoulder. とまる: to perch.
The oni have laid out the treasure they took from the village and are drinking sake. ‖ 並べる: to lay out. 酒: sake, alcohol.
The oni guarding the gate looks sleepy. He's doing nothing but yawn. ‖ 番: guard duty. ねむそう: looking sleepy. 〜ばかり: nothing but ~.
Right, now's our chance. Monkey, can you open the gate? ‖ チャンス: chance. 開けられる: can open.
Leave it to me. When it comes to climbing, I lose to no one. ‖ まかせる: to leave to. 木登り: tree climbing. 負ける: to lose.
The monkey scrambled nimbly up the high wall beside the gate. ‖ わき: side. かべ: wall. するする: smoothly, nimbly.
Then, from the inside, he quietly slid the bolt free. ‖ 内側: inside. そっと: quietly. かんぬき: bolt, bar. 外す: to remove.
With a heavy creak, the iron gate swung open. ‖ 音がする: to make a sound. 開く: to open.
Wh-who's there? What was that noise? ‖ だれだ: who's there!
I am Momotarō, the best in Japan. I've come to take back what you stole from the village! ‖ 日本一: best in Japan. 〜てもらいに来る: to come to have ~ done.
A human child? Don't make me laugh. ‖ 笑わせるな: don't make me laugh.
One after another, the oni came out carrying huge iron clubs. ‖ 金棒: iron club. 次々に: one after another.
But Momotarō and his friends weren't the least bit afraid. ‖ 少しも〜ない: not at all. こわがる: to be afraid.
The pheasant pecked at the oni's eyes from above, the dog bit their legs, and the monkey scratched their backs. ‖ つつく: to peck. かみつく: to bite. ひっかく: to scratch.
Ow, ow, ow! My eyes! I can't see! ‖ いたたた: ouch. 見えない: can't see.
Hey, stop biting my legs! My back hurts too! ‖ 〜な: don't ~. 背中: back.
The oni could do nothing but run around in every direction. ‖ 逃げ回る: to run around fleeing. 〜ばかり: only ~.
Just then, an especially huge oni came stomping out. It was the oni chief. ‖ ひときわ: especially. のっしのっし: stomping heavily. 大将: chief, general.
What's all this racket? It's just one human and three animals, isn't it? ‖ さわぐ: to make a fuss. たかが: merely, just.
Whether it's "just" or not, why don't you try and see. ‖ かどうか: whether or not. 〜てみるがいい: go ahead and try ~.
The chief raised his club high and lunged at Momotarō. ‖ ふり上げる: to swing up. とびかかる: to pounce on.
Momotarō dodged lightly, grabbed the chief's arm, and with a "Hya!" threw him through the air. ‖ ひらりと: nimbly. よける: to dodge. つかむ: to grab. 投げ飛ばす: to hurl.
Thud! The chief fell to the ground, his eyes spinning. ‖ 地面: ground. たおれる: to fall. 目を回す: to be dazed.
I-I give up. I never imagined a human could be this strong. ‖ まいった: I give up. 〜とは思わなかった: never thought that ~.
I'll return all the treasure. I'll send the girls home too. Please, just spare my life. ‖ 帰す: to send home. どうか: please. 命: life.
Do you promise never to ravage the village again? ‖ 二度と〜ない: never again. 約束する: to promise.
I promise. Far from doing evil, from now on I'll do things that help people. ‖ 〜どころか: far from ~. 役に立つ: to be useful.
Momotarō looked steadily into the chief's eyes, and nodded. ‖ じっと: steadily. うなずく: to nod.
Very well. I'll trust your word. ‖ 信じる: to believe.
In a back room, the girls taken from the village were trembling. ‖ 奥: back, interior. 連れてこられる: to be brought. ふるえる: to tremble.
There's no need to worry anymore. Let's go home to the village together. ‖ 〜ことはない: there's no need to ~.
The girls wept for joy. ‖ 〜ながら: while ~. 喜ぶ: to rejoice.
The oni loaded the treasure onto carts and carried it all the way to the boat. ‖ 積む: to load. 〜まで: all the way to ~.
Sacks of rice, gold and silver, coral beads. The boat was so heavy it looked about to sink. ‖ 金銀: gold and silver. さんご: coral. しずむ: to sink. 〜そうなほど: so much that it seemed about to ~.
So much stuff! They sure took a lot. ‖ よく〜ものだ: (exclamation) sure did ~.
It all belongs to the villagers. Don't drop a single thing, okay? ‖ 全部: all. 〜ないように: so as not to ~.
The sea on the way home was unbelievably calm. ‖ うそのように: unbelievably. 静か: calm, quiet.
A great crowd had gathered on the village beach. ‖ 大勢: many people. 集まる: to gather.
They're back! Momotarō is back! ‖ 〜ぞ: (emphasis).
The treasure and the girls, all of them together! ‖ みんないっしょ: all together.
The families whose daughters had been taken hugged each other and couldn't help but cry. ‖ だき合う: to embrace. 〜ずにはいられない: can't help ~ing.
The old man and the old woman came running out of the crowd too. ‖ 人ごみ: crowd.
Momotarō, you made it back safe... I prayed to the gods every day. ‖ 無事: safe. お願いする: to pray, ask.
Not only unhurt, but bringing back the treasure too! You truly are the best in Japan. ‖ 〜うえに: not only ~ but also. 〜まで: even ~. 〜とは: (surprise) to think that ~.
It wasn't my strength alone. It's because the dog, the monkey, and the pheasant were with me. ‖ 〜てくれた: did ~ for me. 〜からです: it's because ~.
The treasure was returned to the villagers, every last piece. ‖ 一つ残らず: every last one. 返される: to be returned.
Afterwards, the dog, the monkey, and the pheasant lived near Momotarō's house and protected the village. ‖ その後: afterwards. 守る: to protect.
The dog and the monkey don't fight anymore. ...Though they say they still do, just a little, now and then. ‖ たまに: now and then. 〜そうだ: they say ~.
Momotarō took good care of the old man and the old woman, and they lived happily ever after. ‖ 大切にする: to cherish. いつまでも: forever.
And so, a happy ending. ‖ めでたし: (story ending) happily concluded.
