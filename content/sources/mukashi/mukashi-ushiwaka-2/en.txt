Cheeky little brat. Don't blame me if you regret it. ‖ 生意気: cheeky. 〜め: (contemptuous) you ~. 後悔: regret. 知らんぞ: I won't care.
Lit by the moon, the two figures on the bridge cast long shadows. ‖ 照らされる: be lit. 影: shadow. のびる: stretch.
Benkei swung his great naginata sideways with a whoosh. ‖ 横に: sideways. ぶうん: whoosh.
In that instant, Ushiwaka's body floated lightly into the air. ‖ 瞬間: instant. ふわりと: lightly. 宙に浮く: float in midair.
Ushiwaka had landed on the bridge railing without making a sound. ‖ らんかん: railing. 音も立てずに: without a sound. 着地: landing.
What? When did you get over there? ‖ いつの間に: before one knows it, when.
Over here. Where are you looking? ‖ こちら: this way.
Benkei turned bright red and this time brought the naginata straight down. ‖ 真っ赤: bright red. 縦に: vertically. 振り下ろす: swing down.
But Ushiwaka nimbly leapt over Benkei's head and came around behind him. ‖ ひらりと: nimbly. 飛びこえる: leap over. 回る: go around.
Then, with the folding fan in his hand, he gave Benkei a light tap on the head. ‖ 扇: folding fan. ぽんと: (light) tap. たたく: hit, tap.
M-me, being teased by a child?! ‖ からかわれる: be teased. 〜とは: (shock) to think ~.
Benkei swung his naginata around again and again. ‖ 振り回す: swing around.
But Ushiwaka, like a butterfly, simply dodged lightly to the right and to the left. ‖ まるで〜のように: just like ~. よける: dodge. 〜ばかり: only ~.
The moves he had learned training with the tengu were far too quick for Benkei's eyes to follow. ‖ 身につける: master, acquire. 追いきれない: can't keep up with.
Sweat came dripping down Benkei's huge body. ‖ 汗: sweat. ぽたぽた: drip drip. 流れ落ちる: run down.
Huff, huff. Stop darting around and running away! Fight fair and square! ‖ ちょこまか: darting about. 〜てばかりいないで: stop just ~ing. 正々堂々: fair and square.
Well then, shall we end this? ‖ そろそろ: soon, about time. 〜にしましょうか: shall we make it ~.
Ushiwaka flung his fan straight at Benkei's face. ‖ 〜に向かって: toward ~. さっと: quickly.
In the moment Benkei shut his eyes without thinking, Ushiwaka kicked the naginata's shaft away. ‖ 思わず: involuntarily. 隙: opening. 柄: handle, shaft. けり飛ばす: kick away.
The naginata spun round and round and fell into the river with a splash. ‖ くるくる: spinning. ぽちゃん: splash.
Before he knew it, the tip of Ushiwaka's sword had stopped dead right in front of Benkei's eyes. ‖ 気がつくと: before one knew it. ぴたりと: dead, exactly.
I-I give up. I've lost. ‖ 参った: I give up. 負け: defeat.
Benkei dropped heavily to his knees on the bridge. ‖ どっかり: heavily. ひざをつく: kneel.
I beat nine hundred ninety-nine warriors, and now I lose to such a small young man? ‖ 〜とは: (shock) to think ~.
Who on earth are you? ‖ いったい: on earth. 何者: who, what kind of person.
I am Ushiwakamaru, son of Minamoto no Yoshitomo. ‖ 〜の子: child of ~.
What! You are the son of that great Genji general? ‖ なんと: what! お子様: child (honorific).
Benkei was silent for a while, but at last he bowed his head deeply. ‖ 黙る: be silent. やがて: before long. 頭を下げる: bow.
Lord Ushiwakamaru, please, make me your retainer. ‖ 〜にしてください: please make (me) ~.
I've realized that serving a truly strong master matters far more than collecting a thousand swords. ‖ 〜より〜ほうが: ~ rather than ~. 仕える: serve.
Give the swords back. You can't become strong with things taken from others. ‖ 〜てあげなさい: (instruction) do ~ for them. 強くはなれない: can't become strong.
Yes. Starting tomorrow, I'll go around returning every single one to its owner. ‖ 一本残らず: every last one. 持ち主: owner. 〜て回る: go around ~ing.
Then from today, you are my retainer. I'm counting on you, Benkei. ‖ よろしく頼む: count on.
And so, in place of a thousandth sword, Ushiwakamaru gained a retainer with the strength of a thousand men. ‖ 〜の代わりに: in place of ~. 千人力: strength of a thousand. 手に入れる: obtain.
Later, Ushiwakamaru grew up and took the name Minamoto no Yoshitsune. ‖ その後: after that. 名乗る: call oneself.
Yoshitsune joined forces with his elder brother Yoritomo and at last defeated the Heike. ‖ 力を合わせる: join forces. ついに: at last.
Charging down cliffs on horseback, leaping from ship to ship, Yoshitsune fought with great agility, just as the tengu had taught him. ‖ 〜たり〜たり: doing things like ~ and ~. 身軽: agile. 〜とおり: just as ~.
Throughout the battles, Benkei never left Yoshitsune's side and kept protecting his master. ‖ 離れずに: without leaving. 〜続ける: keep ~ing.
But Yoshitsune, having won too much glory, came to be distrusted by his brother Yoritomo. ‖ 手がらを立てる: achieve merit. 〜すぎる: too much. 疑われる: be suspected.
Protecting Yoshitsune, now a hunted man, Benkei fought to the very end. ‖ 追われる身: a hunted person. 最後まで: to the end.
It is said that even with arrows all over his body, Benkei did not fall, but protected his master while standing. ‖ 矢: arrow. 〜まま: while still ~. 〜と言われている: it is said that.
From this came the expression "Benkei's standing death" (Benkei no tachi-ōjō). ‖ 立ち往生: dying on one's feet; being stuck. 生まれる: be born.
Even today, it is used for being stuck, unable to go forward or back. ‖ 進めない: can't advance. 困る: be in trouble.
Benkei's name lives on in many other expressions, too. ‖ 残る: remain.
For example, the shin at the front of the leg is called "Benkei's weeping spot". ‖ すね: shin. 泣き所: weak spot.
It means that even someone as strong as Benkei would cry if he hit it there. ‖ 〜でも: even ~. 〜ほど: so much that ~.
Someone who acts tough at home but is meek outside is called an "uchi-Benkei" (a Benkei at home). ‖ いばる: be bossy. おとなしい: meek, quiet.
And the feeling of wanting to cheer for the pitiful or the underdog is called "hōgan-biiki". ‖ 応援: cheering, support. ひいき: favoritism.
Hōgan was Yoshitsune's official title. It comes from how many people sided with Yoshitsune, who met a sad end. ‖ 役職: official post. 最後をむかえる: meet one's end.
Eight hundred years later, their names still live on in everyday Japanese. ‖ たつ: pass. 生きる: live.
By the Gojō Great Bridge in Kyoto, there is still a statue of Ushiwakamaru and Benkei. ‖ 像: statue.
If you go to Kyoto, be sure to go and see the two of them. ‖ ぜひ: by all means. 〜に行ってみてください: try going to ~.
Is there an "uchi-Benkei" around you? ‖ まわり: surroundings.
What is true strength? The meeting of these two still teaches us that today. ‖ 出会い: encounter. 〜てくれる: ~ for us.
And that is the end of the tale of Ushiwakamaru and Benkei. ‖ おしまい: the end.
