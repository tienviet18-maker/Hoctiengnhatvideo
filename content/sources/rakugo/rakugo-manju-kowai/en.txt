In Edo's row houses there was a room where young men gathered to chat after work. ‖ 長屋 (nagaya): long row houses shared by ordinary families in Edo. 〜と: "whenever ~". おしゃべり: chatting.
One summer night, some idle men got together and started talking about "the scariest thing in the world." ‖ 暇な: "idle, free". 〜の話を始める: "start talking about ~".
Hey, everyone. Everybody has at least one thing they can't stand, right? ‖ 苦手: "bad with, afraid of" — softer than 嫌い, very common (数学が苦手). 〜ぐらい: "at least about ~".
Me, I really can't stand snakes. Just seeing that long thin body sends a chill down my back. ‖ 大の苦手: "really can't stand". 〜だけで: "just by ~". 背中が寒くなる: "a chill runs down the spine".
For me it's spiders. When one suddenly drops from the ceiling, I can't help yelling. ‖ 思わず: "without meaning to". 〜ちまう: Edo-style 〜てしまう.
Kuma, you're so big, and you're scared of spiders? ‖ 〜のに: "even though" — surprise at something unexpected.
Size has nothing to do with it. Those thin legs — all eight of them moving! ‖ 関係ない: "irrelevant". 本 counts long things; 八本 is read はっぽん. 〜ぞ: emphatic, masculine.
Now that you say it, that is creepy. ‖ そう言われると: "when you put it that way". 気味が悪い: "creepy".
The others took turns too: afraid of ants, afraid of frogs. ‖ 次々に: "one after another". 〜と話す quotes what was said.
But in a corner of the room, one man was just sitting there quietly grinning. It was Matsu. ‖ ところが: "however" (unexpected turn). 黙って: "silently, without a word".
Hey, Matsu. What've you been grinning about this whole time? ‖ さっきから: "since a while ago". 〜んだい: casual Edo question.
Oh, I just think you're all pathetic. Snakes, spiders — they don't scare me one bit. ‖ 情けない: "pathetic, shameful". ちっとも〜ない: "not at all".
Really? So there's nothing at all you're afraid of? ‖ 一つも〜ない: "not even one". お前: "you" — casual between men, never to superiors.
I'd like to say nothing… but actually, there is just one thing. ‖ 〜と言いたいところだけど: "I'd love to say ~, but". 実は: "actually" — very common when confessing.
What? What is it? Stop teasing and tell us already. ‖ もったいぶる: "to make a big deal, hold back". 〜ないで: "without ~ / don't ~". 教えろ: blunt command.
I don't want to say it. Just remembering it makes me feel sick. ‖ 思い出す: "to recall". 気分が悪い: "to feel sick/unwell".
Come on, just say it. We won't laugh. ‖ いいから: "never mind that, just ~". 〜からさ: reassuring "because…".
…Manjū. ‖ まんじゅう: steamed buns filled with sweet bean paste, sold all over Japan and popular as souvenirs.
Manjū? Those sweet round buns? ‖ Repeating to confirm. 〜かい: casual question.
That's right. Just hearing the name makes me shake. I can't take it. I'll go lie down in the next room. ‖ 震えてくる: "start to tremble". 横になる: "to lie down" — also heard at clinics.
Matsu went pale and left for the next room. ‖ 青い顔をする: "to turn pale". 〜てしまいました: went off.
The men left behind looked at each other and began to smirk. ‖ 顔を見合わせる: "to exchange looks". にやにや: a scheming grin.
Did you hear that? He's scared of manjū, he says. ‖ 〜んだってよ: "I hear that ~", passing on what someone said.
Matsu always acts so high and mighty. Let's teach him a little lesson. ‖ 偉そうに: "arrogantly". 懲らしめる: "to teach a lesson". 〜てやろうじゃないか: "let's go do ~ to him".
Good idea. Let's all chip in and buy a mountain of manjū. ‖ 出し合う: "to pool, chip in". 山ほど: "heaps of".
The men ran into town and bought up manjū from all kinds of shops. ‖ 買い集める = 買う + 集める: "to buy up, collect".
Chestnut manjū, buckwheat manjū, hot-spring manjū. A big tray was piled full of them. ‖ 温泉まんじゅう: manjū sold in hot-spring towns, a classic onsen souvenir.
Among them were rare manjū sold only on festival days. ‖ 〜にしか〜ない: "only ~". 珍しい: "rare".
All right, let's put all of these by Matsu's pillow. ‖ 枕元: "by the pillow, bedside". 〜てこよう: "go do ~ (and come back)".
When he wakes up, he'll surely be scared stiff. ‖ 腰を抜かす: "to be too scared to stand". きっと: "surely".
They quietly set the manjū down, hid behind the sliding door, and watched. ‖ こっそり: "secretly". ふすま: thick paper sliding door. 様子をうかがう: "to watch what happens".
After a while, Matsu woke up. ‖ 目を覚ます: "to wake up". しばらくすると: "after a while".
Waah! Manjū! So scary, so scary! ‖ Is the scream real or fake? Keep listening.
Hear that? He's screaming. ‖ 叫んでる: spoken short form of 叫んでいる.
So many… how scary. This chestnut one is especially scary. ‖ 〜なんて: picks an example with strong feeling. 特に: "especially".
But Matsu's voice grew quieter and quieter. ‖ だんだん: "gradually". 〜ていきました: ongoing change.
Hey, he's gone quiet. Don't tell me he fainted? ‖ もしかして: "could it be…". 気を失う: "to faint".
Worried, the men peeked through a gap in the sliding door, and… ‖ のぞいてみると: "when they took a peek". すきま: "gap".
So scary. Munch, munch. This buckwheat one too, oh so scary. Chomp, chomp. ‖ むしゃむしゃ, もぐもぐ: sounds of happy eating.
Ah! He's eating the manjū! ‖ あっ！: a gasp of realization.
The hot-spring manjū has such moist skin — even scarier. ‖ しっとり: "moist, soft" — a common compliment for sweets or skin.
We've been tricked! He actually loves manjū! ‖ だまされた: passive of だます, "to be fooled".
The men flung the door open and burst into the room. ‖ 勢いよく: "forcefully". 飛び込む: "to dash in".
Hey, Matsu! You tricked us! ‖ こら: a scolding shout. 〜たな: accusing tone.
I didn't trick anyone. I really was scared. Scared of how delicious they were. ‖ 〜なんかいない: strong denial. 〜すぎて: "because too ~".
So what are you really afraid of? Tell us honestly! ‖ 正直に: "honestly". 言え: command form of 言う.
Matsu swallowed the last manjū, smiled broadly, and said: ‖ 飲み込む: "to swallow". にっこり: "with a big smile".
Let me see. Right now, I'm afraid of a nice cup of hot tea. ‖ This is the punch line (ochi). 一杯: "one cup".
After eating sweets, you want hot tea, don't you? That's what he called "scary." ‖ 〜たくなる: "come to want ~". Japanese often pair green tea with traditional sweets.
A story of Edo wit and humor: using a fake "I'm scared" to get what you want. ‖ 手に入れる: "to obtain". 知恵: "wisdom, wit".
Even today in Japan, people jokingly say "manjū kowai" about something they actually love. ‖ 冗談で: "as a joke". 〜ことがある: "sometimes ~".
The next story is about a lord who had never once eaten fish from the sea. ‖ お殿様: "lord". 〜たことのない: "never having ~".
