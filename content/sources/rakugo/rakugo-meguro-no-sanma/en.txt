Sanma (Pacific saury) is a long, thin, cheap fish eaten all over Japan in autumn. ‖ 〜と: "when it becomes ~". 食べられる: passive, "is eaten". さんま: salt-grilled saury is a classic autumn dish.
In the Edo period, sanma was the townspeople's fish. It was not the kind of fish a lord would eat. ‖ 〜ような: "the kind of ~". 〜ではなかったのです: explains the background.
Today's story is about a lord who fell in love with that very sanma. ‖ 恋をする: "to fall in love". 〜てしまった: couldn't help it.
One clear autumn day, the lord took his retainers and went falconing in Meguro. ‖ 〜を連れて: "taking ~ along". 鷹狩り: hunting with hawks, a samurai pastime.
Meguro is now in the middle of Tokyo, but back then it was countryside, all fields and woods. ‖ 当時: "at that time". 〜ばかり: "nothing but ~". Meguro today is a busy Tokyo ward.
Absorbed in riding his horse, the lord became completely starving before he knew it. ‖ 夢中で: "absorbed". 〜うちに: "while ~ (before one knows)". すっかり: "completely".
You there, I'm hungry. Is there nothing to eat? ‖ 腹が減った: "I'm hungry" (blunt, masculine). これ: calling a subordinate.
I am deeply sorry, my lord. We left in a hurry and forgot the lunch. ‖ 申し訳ございません: very formal apology, common at work. 〜てまいりました: humble 〜てきました.
Just then, a wonderful smell came drifting from somewhere. ‖ においがする: "there is a smell". 〜てきました: drifting toward them.
What is that smell? It smells irresistibly delicious. ‖ 〜じゃ: old-fashioned speech of elders/nobles. たまらなく: "irresistibly". 〜そう: "looks/seems ~".
That is the smell of a fish called sanma being grilled, my lord. A cheap fish eaten by common folk… ‖ 下々の者: "the lower classes" (archaic). 〜でございます: very polite です.
No matter. I wish to eat it. Bring it at once. ‖ かまわぬ: "it doesn't matter" (archaic). 余: "I" (a lord's word). まいれ: command form of humble 参る.
The retainer ran to a nearby farmhouse and asked an old woman. ‖ 頼む: "to ask a favor". 農家: "farmhouse".
His lordship, eating our sanma? Would something like this really be all right? ‖ よろしいんでしょうか: "would it be all right?" — polite, heard in shops.
The old woman served the sanma grilled over charcoal until it was black, just as it was, on a plate. ‖ 炭火: "charcoal fire". 〜まで: "until ~". そのまま: "as it is".
The skin was crispy, and the fat sizzled. ‖ ぱりぱり: "crispy". じゅうじゅう: sizzling sound. 音を立てる: "to make a sound".
Mm… delicious! Never in my life have I eaten such delicious fish! ‖ 生まれて初めて: "for the first time in my life" — a very common exclamation.
The lord ate it clean, right down to around the bones. ‖ きれいに食べる: "to eat cleanly" — eating fish clean to the bone is good manners in Japan.
Old woman, well done. Such a taste has never once been served at the castle. ‖ 大儀であった: archaic praise to a subordinate. 〜たことがない: "have never ~".
Thank you, my lord. Fresh off the grill is always the best. ‖ 焼きたて: "freshly grilled" (〜たて: just made, as in できたて, 炊きたて).
Even after returning to the castle, the lord could not forget that taste. ‖ 忘れられない: "can't forget". 〜てからも: "even after ~".
But sanma never appeared in the castle's meals. ‖ 〜ことはありません: "it never happens that ~".
If he said "I want sanma," his retainers might laugh at him. ‖ 〜ば: conditional. 笑われる: passive, "be laughed at". 〜かもしれません: "might".
The lord longed for sanma so much that he dreamed of it every night. ‖ 〜ほど: "so much that ~". 会いたくなる: "come to miss" — said of the fish like a sweetheart.
My lord, you have not seemed well lately. Is something wrong with your health? ‖ 近ごろ: "lately". 具合が悪い: "unwell" — used at clinics and work.
No, it's nothing. …I was just remembering that taste in Meguro. ‖ 何でもない: "it's nothing". 〜ておった: archaic 〜ていた.
One day, it was arranged that a relative's house would hold a feast. ‖ 〜ことになりました: "it was decided/arranged that ~" (not by oneself).
My lord, they say that today you may order anything you like. ‖ お申しつけください: "please give your orders" — honorific, heard in hotels. 〜とのことです: "I'm told that ~".
Anything at all? Then… bring me sanma. ‖ 何でもいい: "anything is fine". では: "then".
The relative's cooks were shocked. They had never served such a cheap fish to a lord. ‖ びっくりする: "to be startled". 〜たことはない: "have never".
The cooks hurried to the Nihonbashi fish market and bought the very best sanma. ‖ 日本橋: Edo's commercial heart, once home to the largest fish market.
Then they talked it over. Too much fat might be bad for his lordship's health. ‖ 相談する: "to consult" — very common at work. 体に悪い: "bad for your health".
And if a little bone got stuck in his throat, it would be a disaster. ‖ 刺さる: "to get stuck, pierce". 〜たら: "if (it happened)". 大変だ: "terrible".
So they steamed the sanma to remove all the fat, and carefully took out every bone, one by one. ‖ そこで: "so, therefore". 抜く: "to remove". ていねいに: "carefully".
It fell to pieces, so they rolled it into balls like dumplings and served it in a bowl. ‖ ばらばら: "in pieces". 〜のように: "like ~". 丸くする: "to make round".
This is sanma? Its shape has changed quite a bit. ‖ ずいぶん: "quite". 〜のう: archaic sentence ending (= ね).
The lord took one bite and tilted his head, puzzled. ‖ 首をかしげる: "to tilt one's head" — the Japanese gesture of doubt.
…It has no taste. The sanma I ate in Meguro back then was far more fragrant and rich. ‖ 味がせぬ = 味がしない (archaic). 香ばしい: "fragrant (roasted)". 脂がのる: "rich, fatty" (fish in season).
You there, where did you obtain this sanma? ‖ 手に入れる: "to obtain". 〜のじゃ: old-fashioned question.
Yes, my lord, it is the finest sanma, bought from the Nihonbashi fish market. ‖ 上等: "top quality".
Nihonbashi? That won't do. ‖ いかん: "that won't do" (= いけない).
The lord declared firmly: ‖ はっきり: "clearly, firmly".
Sanma has to be from Meguro. ‖ 〜に限る: "nothing beats ~". This famous punch line (ochi) is still used as a joke.
Meguro is countryside far from the sea. Sanma isn't caught in any "sea of Meguro." ‖ 〜わけではありません: "it's not that ~". とれる: "to be caught (fish)".
The lord knew nothing about the sea, or about cooking, did he? ‖ 〜も〜も: "neither ~ nor ~". 〜のですね: gentle conclusion.
What was truly delicious was the plain sanma, fresh off the charcoal. ‖ 〜たばかり: "just done". 飾らない: "unpretentious, plain".
Even today, every autumn Meguro in Tokyo holds the "Meguro Sanma Festival," where sanma is handed out for free. ‖ 開かれる: passive, "is held". 無料で: "for free". 配る: "to hand out".
