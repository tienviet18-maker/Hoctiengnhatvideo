Long ago, a slow-going young man named Yotarō came to help out at a secondhand shop in Edo. ‖ 古道具屋: secondhand shop. のんびり: easygoing, slow. 手伝い: helping out.
One rainy day, while his uncle was out, Yotarō was minding the shop alone. ‖ 〜あいだ: while. 店番: minding the shop.
Excuse me. It suddenly started raining — could you lend me an umbrella? ‖ 急に: suddenly. 傘: umbrella. 〜ていただけませんか: (polite) could you ~ for me?
Sure. Here, please take this new umbrella. ‖ 持っていく: to take along.
Yotarō went and lent the finest umbrella in the shop to a stranger. ‖ 上等: top quality. 〜てしまう: go and ~.
When his uncle came back and heard about it, he was appalled. ‖ あきれる: to be appalled.
You fool. If you lend a new umbrella to a stranger, there's no way it'll come back. ‖ 返る: to come back. 〜はずがない: no way ~.
Then what should I have said? ‖ 〜ばよかった: should have ~.
At times like that, you turn them down: "Our umbrellas got their ribs smashed in the wind the other day, so they can't be used." ‖ 骨: rib (of umbrella), bone. ばらばら: in pieces. 断る: to refuse.
I see. The ribs are smashed. I'll remember that well. ‖ なるほど: I see. 〜ておく: do ~ for later.
Yotarō kept repeating under his breath, "ribs are smashed, ribs are smashed." ‖ 口の中で: under one's breath. くり返す: to repeat.
The next day, this time the woman next door came by. ‖ となり: next door. おかみさん: housewife, missus.
Yotarō, could you lend me your cat for a little while? ‖ 〜てくれないかしら: (feminine) could you ~ for me?
Mice keep showing up in my kitchen, and I'm at my wits' end. ‖ 台所: kitchen. ねずみ: mouse.
Yotarō remembered what he'd been taught the day before. ‖ 教わる: to be taught. 思い出す: to recall.
I'm sorry, but our cat got its bones smashed in the wind the other day, so it can't be used. ‖ あいにく: unfortunately.
The cat's bones are smashed? How terrible. Poor thing. ‖ かわいそう: pitiful.
The woman went home, tilting her head in puzzlement. ‖ 首をかしげる: to tilt one's head (puzzled).
Hearing this, the uncle held his head in his hands again. ‖ 頭をかかえる: to hold one's head.
There's no way a cat's bones get smashed. When it's a cat, there's a different way of refusing. ‖ 〜わけがない: there's no way ~. 〜方: way of ~ing.
You just say, "Our cat went into heat and wandered off somewhere." ‖ さかりがつく: to go into heat.
I see. Umbrellas have smashed ribs, cats go into heat, right? ‖ 〜ですね: right?
Got it. This time I won't get it wrong. ‖ まちがえる: to make a mistake.
That afternoon, the uncle went out again on an errand. ‖ 用事: errand.
Only Yotarō and his aunt were left in the shop. ‖ 叔母: aunt. 残る: to remain.
Just then, a fast-talking messenger, said to be from Osaka, came in. ‖ 〜という: said to be. 早口: fast talking. 使い: messenger.
Good day, much obliged. I'm from the place of Yaichi, the go-between dealer in Osaka. ‖ まいど、おおきに: (Osaka) hello, thanks. わて: (Osaka) I. 中買: middleman dealer. 〜でおます: (Osaka) = です.
Huh? What did you say? ‖ 何ですって: what did you say?
At a loss, Yotarō went inside and fetched his aunt. ‖ 奥: inner part of the house. 呼んでくる: to go and call.
I'm so sorry. Could you say that once more, slowly? ‖ もう一度: once more. ゆっくり: slowly.
Yes, certainly. Well then, let me say it once more. ‖ よろしおま: (Osaka) certainly. ほな: (Osaka) well then. もう一ぺん: once more.
But the man started talking even faster than before. ‖ さっきよりも: than before. しゃべる: to talk.
About the items you asked for the other day — the kinmeichiku bamboo vase and the "old pond" hanging scroll — both are fine pieces. ‖ 先日: the other day. 件: matter. 花入れ: flower vase. ええ: (Osaka) = いい.
But that Yaichi, they were such fine pieces he got so happy his head was spinning. ‖ さかい: (Osaka) = から. うれしゅうて: = うれしくて. 目を回す: to get dizzy.
Next thing you know he staggers into the garden and — splash! — jumps right into the old pond. ‖ 〜と思たら: (Osaka) no sooner ~ than. どぼーん: splash. 〜よりましてん: (Osaka, disparaging) went and ~.
So please tell your master he'll get a reply another time. Well, goodbye. ‖ 改めて: another time. 伝える: to pass on. さいなら: (Osaka) goodbye.
W-wait a moment! ‖ ちょっと待って: wait a moment.
The man said all that at tremendous speed and left in a hurry. ‖ ものすごい: tremendous. さっさと: quickly.
Auntie, what did that man just say? ‖ 何て: (casual) = 何と.
Who knows. I only understood about half of it myself. ‖ 半分: half. 〜しか〜ない: only.
A little later, the uncle came back. ‖ しばらくして: after a while.
Did anyone come while I was out? ‖ 留守: being away.
Yes. But I didn't understand a thing. ‖ 何も〜ない: nothing.
Yes, a messenger came from Osaka. But he talked so fast I couldn't really understand. ‖ よく分からない: not really understand.
Just tell me the parts you understood. ‖ 〜だけでいい: just ~ is fine. 〜てごらん: try ~.
Um, they said someone called Yaichi-something got dizzy. ‖ 何とか: something or other. 〜そうです: they say.
Yaichi? That go-between dealer Yaichi? ‖ あの: that (known).
And then he staggered into the garden and jumped into the old pond, he said. ‖ 〜と言っていました: was saying that.
What, jumped into the old pond? That's terrible. Was he hurt? ‖ けが: injury.
Who knows — he didn't say. ‖ そこまでは: not that far.
Does everyone in Osaka talk that fast? ‖ あんなに: that much.
You keep quiet. ‖ 黙る: to keep quiet.
Wait a minute. Speaking of the old pond, this must be about the "Old Pond" scroll I asked for. ‖ 待てよ: wait a minute. 〜と言えば: speaking of ~. 頼んでおく: to order in advance.
It's a fine picture I've wanted for ages. So, was it decided that we'd buy that scroll? ‖ 欲しい: to want. 〜ことに決まる: be decided that ~.
No, it's still "kawazu" (not bought). ‖ 買わず: = 買わない, not buying (old form); sounds like 蛙 (かわず).
"Kawazu," eh? I see — what jumped into the old pond wasn't Yaichi, it was a frog! ‖ 蛙: frog. 〜わけだ: so that's why, no wonder.
There's a famous haiku: "An old pond — a frog jumps in, the sound of water." ‖ 古池や蛙飛びこむ水の音: haiku by Matsuo Bashō. 俳句: haiku.
The aunt's "kawazu" (not bought) sounds the same as "kawazu", an old word for frog. ‖ 表す: to express. 同じ音: same sound.
Uncle, I don't understand any of it. ‖ さっぱり〜ない: not at all.
That's fine. You just remember how to refuse for umbrellas and cats. ‖ 覚えておけ: remember (command).
Yes, sir. Umbrellas went into heat and wandered off. Cats have smashed bones. ‖ さかりがつく: to go into heat.
Backwards, backwards! You still haven't remembered a thing, have you? ‖ 逆: the opposite. やっぱり: just as I thought.
That day, the shop rang with the uncle's big sighs and Yotarō's carefree laughter. ‖ ため息: sigh. のんき: carefree. ひびく: to echo.
