In the town of Edo lived a man called Hachigorō. He was always thinking about nothing but sake. ‖ 〜という: called ~. 〜ばかり: nothing but ~. 考えています: thinks (habit).
One day, Hachigorō went to visit the knowledgeable old Goinkyo. ‖ 物知り: a knowledgeable person. 〜へ遊びに行く: go visit ~.
A "goinkyo" is an elderly person who has retired and takes it easy at home. ‖ 〜というのは〜のことです: ~ means ~. お年寄り: elderly person (polite).
Goinkyo, I heard you can drink sake for free. Is that true? ‖ ただで: for free. 〜って聞いた: I heard ~. 〜んですが: polite lead-in to a question.
Free sake? There's no such sweet deal. But I do have one good idea. ‖ うまい話: a too-good-to-be-true deal. 〜なら: if it's ~.
You praise people. Praise them well, and they're pleased and treat you. ‖ 〜ば: if ~. ほめられた人: the person praised (passive). ごちそうする: treat someone to food or drink.
Wow. Just by praising, I can drink sake? Please teach me. ‖ 〜だけで: just by ~. ぜひ: by all means.
First, to someone you haven't seen in a while, say "You look well." ‖ 久しぶりに: after a long time. 元気そう: look well (〜そう: seem).
Then ask their age, and say a younger age than the real one. ‖ 年を聞く: ask someone's age. 〜より: than ~. 若く言う: say younger.
Say younger? ‖ Repeating to check.
That's right. To someone who's forty-five, say "You look about forty." ‖ 〜くらい: about ~. 〜に見える: look ~.
Everyone is happy to be told they look young. ‖ 誰でも: anyone. 言われる: be told (passive). 〜ものだ: that's how things are.
I see. Then how do you praise a baby? ‖ なるほど: I see. どうやって: how.
For a baby, just say "He takes after his grandpa — he'll live a long life." ‖ 〜に似て: takes after ~. 長生きしそう: looks like he'll live long. 〜ばいい: you just have to ~.
"He takes after his dad — such a clever face" works too. ‖ かしこそう: looks clever. 〜でもいい: ~ is fine too.
Got it! I'll try it right away! ‖ すぐに: right away. 〜てみます: try ~.
Hachigorō said his thanks and hurried outside. ‖ お礼を言う: say thanks. 〜と: as soon as ~. 急いで: in a hurry.
Just then, a bantō he knew well came walking toward him. ‖ ちょうど: just then. 向こうから: from the other way. 歩いてくる: come walking.
A "bantō" is the most senior clerk in a shop. ‖ 一番上の: top, highest. 店員: shop clerk.
Hello, Bantō-san! You look well! ‖ やあ: hi (casual). 〜そうですね: you look ~.
Ah, Hattsan. Yes, I'm well, thank you. ‖ 八っつぁん: friendly name for Hachigorō. おかげさまで: thanks to you (polite reply).
By the way, Bantō-san, how old are you this year? ‖ ところで: by the way. おいくつ: how old (polite).
Me? I'm forty-five this year. ‖ 〜かい: question (friendly). 今年で: as of this year.
Forty-five! In that case… um, what was I supposed to say? ‖ ええと: um. 〜んだっけ: what was it again?
Hachigorō had already half forgotten what the Goinkyo taught him. ‖ 習ったこと: what he learned. 〜かける: half ~, start to ~.
Forty-five, huh. …Any way you look at it, you look forty-five! ‖ どう見ても: any way you look at it.
Well, I am forty-five. Now, I'm in a hurry, so goodbye. ‖ そりゃあ: well, of course. これで: that's all (parting).
The bantō walked off, tilting his head in puzzlement. ‖ 首をかしげる: tilt one's head (puzzled). 〜ながら: while ~.
Huh, that's odd. No sake is coming out. ‖ おかしい: strange. 出てこない: doesn't appear. 〜ぞ: emphasis (male).
Hachigorō thought for a while. ‖ しばらく: for a while. 考える: think.
Oops, I forgot to say younger. Next time I'll get it right. ‖ しまった: oops. 〜のを忘れた: forgot to ~. うまくやる: do it well.
So Hachigorō went to his friend Take's house. ‖ そこで: so. 友だち: friend.
At Take's house, a baby had just been born a week before. ‖ 〜たばかり: just ~. 一週間前に: a week ago.
Hey, Take, I hear you've had a baby! Congratulations! ‖ 〜んだって: I hear ~. おめでとう: congratulations.
Hey, Hachi, you came! Thanks. He's sleeping in the next room. ‖ 来てくれた: came (for me). となり: next door.
When Hachigorō looked into the next room, someone was sleeping under the futon. ‖ 〜と: when ~ (discovery). 布団: futon. 誰か: someone.
Oh, so this is the baby. …He's pretty big. ‖ ずいぶん: quite.
Lots of wrinkles on his face, and his head's all shiny. He looks just like his grandpa! ‖ しわ: wrinkles. つるつる: smooth, shiny. 〜に似ている: look like ~.
Hey, that's my old man. He's been asleep since noon. ‖ うちのじいさん: my old man (Take's father). 昼から: since noon.
Huh, your old man? Sorry about that. ‖ えっ: huh (surprise). 失礼: excuse me, how rude of me.
The baby's over here. In the small futon, see? ‖ こっち: here. 〜だろう: see?
Oh, over here. He's small. ‖ 〜か: oh (realizing).
Um… he takes after his grandpa — looks like he'll live a long life! ‖ 〜に似て: takes after ~. 長生きしそう: looks like he'll live long.
Well, thanks. But you just mistook my old man for him, didn't you? ‖ 間違えたばかり: just mistook. 〜じゃないか: didn't you? (reproach).
No, that was… Oh right, I was supposed to ask the age. ‖ 〜んだった: I was supposed to ~ (remembering).
By the way, Take, how old is this little one? ‖ この子: this child. おいくつ: how old.
He's just a week old. ‖ 生まれて: since being born.
A week, huh. For a week old, he… um… ‖ 〜にしては: for ~ (contrary to expectation).
Hachigorō tried to recall the Goinkyo's words. Say younger than the real age. ‖ 思い出そうとする: try to recall. 〜のです: explanation.
In the Edo period they used "kazoe-doshi": a baby counted as one year old at birth. ‖ 数え年: traditional age counting. 〜と数える: count as ~.
By kazoe-doshi, he's one. ‖ 〜なら: if counting by ~.
One! For one, he looks awfully young. ‖ 〜にしては: for ~. お若く見える: look young (honorific).
Young? He's a baby! So how old does he look? ‖ 〜ぞ: emphasis. いくつに見える: how old does he look?
Any way you look at it… he looks "tada"! ‖ ただ: zero / free (pun).
Tada? What's that supposed to mean? ‖ なんだい: what is it (casual).
Younger than one means zero. In other words, "tada" (nothing). ‖ つまり: in other words. 〜というわけです: that's the idea.
"Tada" also means "costing no money", free. ‖ お金がかからない: costs nothing. 意味: meaning.
A perfect "ochi" for Hachigorō, who wanted free sake. ‖ ちょうどいい: just right. おち: punchline.
Praising people may be harder than you think. ‖ 思ったより: than you think. 〜かもしれません: may.
