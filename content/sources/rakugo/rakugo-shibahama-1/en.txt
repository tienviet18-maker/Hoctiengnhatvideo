In the Shiba district of Edo lived a fishmonger named Katsugorō. ‖ 〜という: named ~. 魚屋: fishmonger, fish shop. 〜がいました: there was (a person).
He had the best eye for fish in Edo. But Katsugorō had one troublesome habit. ‖ 見る目: eye for, judgment. 困ったくせ: a bad habit.
He loved sake too much, and once he started drinking he couldn't stop. ‖ 〜すぎて: too ~, so. 〜始める: start ~. 〜のです: explaining.
The day after drinking, he couldn't get up in the morning and skipped work. ‖ 起きられず = 起きられないで: unable to get up. 〜てしまいます: ends up ~ (bad habit).
One by one, his customers went off to other fishmongers. ‖ 一人、また一人と: one, then another. 〜てしまいました: went away (regret).
This went on until there was no money left in the house even to buy rice. ‖ 続く: continue. もう〜ない: no more ~.
Hey, dear, get up. Today of all days, please go to the fish market. ‖ おまえさん: how an Edo wife addressed her husband. 〜こそ: this very ~. 〜ておくれ: please ~ (old style).
Ugh… my head hurts. Make it tomorrow. ‖ 痛え = 痛い (rough Edo speech). 〜にしてくれ: make it ~.
Tomorrow, tomorrow, you've already taken twenty days off. And we're out of rice, too. ‖ 〜って: quoting what someone keeps saying. 二十日 (はつか): twenty days. 〜んだから: you know (reproach).
All right, all right. I just have to go, don't I? ‖ 〜ばいいんだろう: I just have to ~, right? (annoyed).
Reluctantly, Katsugorō shouldered his tools and went out into the still-dark streets. ‖ しぶしぶ: reluctantly. かつぐ: carry on the shoulder.
The winter morning wind blew cold against his face. ‖ 当たる: hit, blow against. 冷たく: adjective → adverb.
When he reached Shiba beach, every stall at the fish market was still closed. ‖ 〜と: when ~. どこもまだ: everywhere still.
What, nobody's here. …She got the time wrong and woke me a whole koku early. ‖ いねえ = いない. 〜やがった: rough ending showing annoyance. 一刻: an old unit of time.
One koku is about two hours today. Katsugorō decided to wait on the beach for dawn. ‖ 〜ほど: about ~. 〜ことにしました: decided to ~.
Can't be helped. I'll take a break here. ‖ しょうがねえ = しょうがない: it can't be helped. 〜か: I guess I'll ~ (to oneself).
As he washed his face in the sea and had a smoke, the sky gradually grew lighter. ‖ 一服する: have a smoke, take a short break. だんだん: gradually. 〜てきました: becoming ~.
Just then, he saw something long and thin among the waves. ‖ 波: wave. 細長い: long and thin. 見える: be visible.
What's that? A cord? …No, it's heavy. ‖ ありゃ = あれは. ひも: string, cord.
He pulled it up, and it was an old leather wallet. ‖ 引っ張り上げる: pull up. 〜てみると: when (one) tried ~. 革: leather.
Peeking inside, Katsugorō nearly cried out. ‖ のぞく: peek. 思わず: involuntarily. 〜そうになる: nearly ~.
The wallet was packed with gold koban coins. ‖ 小判: oval Edo gold coin. ぎっしり: tightly packed.
Katsugorō tucked the wallet into the front of his kimono and ran home. ‖ 懐: inside the front of a kimono. 〜と: as soon as ~.
Hey, shut the door! Quick! ‖ 閉めろ: imperative of 閉める (rough).
What's wrong? Where's the fish? Didn't you go to the market? ‖ 〜かい: old-style casual question. 河岸: riverside fish market.
Forget the fish. Look at this. ‖ 〜どころじゃない: this is no time for ~. 見ろ: imperative of 見る.
When the two of them counted it, there were forty-two ryō in all. ‖ 両: ryō, Edo gold currency. 全部で: in total.
One ryō is worth hundreds of thousands of yen today. Forty-two ryō was an incredible fortune. ‖ 何十万円: hundreds of thousands of yen. とんでもない: incredible, outrageous.
A fortune like this… what on earth are you going to do with it? ‖ いったい: on earth. 〜つもり: intend to ~.
With this, I don't have to work anymore. I can drink as much sake as I like every day. ‖ 〜なくていい: don't need to ~. 好きなだけ: as much as one likes. 飲める: can drink.
Dear, you can't… ‖ そんな……: trailing off, showing worry or protest.
Today we celebrate! Go buy some sake! ‖ お祝い: celebration. 〜てこい: go and ~ (rough command).
Katsugorō called in his friends from the neighborhood and drank heavily from the morning on. ‖ 大酒を飲む: drink heavily.
Kattsu, you're really flush today, aren't you? ‖ 勝っつぁん: Edo nickname for Katsu. 景気がいい: business is good, flush with money.
Yeah, don't hold back. Drink up. If it's money, I've got plenty. ‖ 遠慮する: hold back. どんどん: keep on. 〜なら: if it's ~.
After partying until night, Katsugorō fell asleep right there, snoring. ‖ 騒ぐ: make noise, party. いびきをかく: snore. そのまま: just as he was.
His wife sat alone with a dark face, thinking about something. ‖ 暗い顔: a gloomy face. 何か: something.
The next morning, Katsugorō was woken by his wife. ‖ 起こされる: be woken (passive). 女房: wife (old-fashioned).
Dear, get up. Please go to the market. ‖ 〜ておくれよ: please ~ (old-style, wife to husband).
What are you saying? We've got the wallet, haven't we? The one with forty-two ryō. ‖ 〜じゃねえか = 〜じゃないか: haven't we?
Wallet? What are you talking about? ‖ 何の話だい: what are you talking about (old casual).
The wallet I picked up on Shiba beach yesterday morning. ‖ 拾う: pick up. The clause before 財布 describes it.
Are you still half asleep? You didn't go to the market yesterday. ‖ 寝ぼける: be half asleep. 〜なんか〜ない: didn't ~ at all (strong denial).
You slept till noon, and the moment you got up you called your friends, drank, and fell asleep again, didn't you? ‖ 〜たと思ったら: no sooner ~ than. 寝ちまった = 寝てしまった (Edo speech).
That can't be. …Then what about the wallet? ‖ そんなはずはない: that can't be.
Katsugorō's face slowly turned pale. ‖ 青くなる: turn pale. 〜ていきました: gradually ~.
You must have dreamed it. You keep wishing for money, so you dream like that. ‖ 〜でも: or something. 〜のさ: assertive ending (old casual).
Katsugorō searched the house. But the wallet was nowhere to be found. ‖ 探す: search. どこにもない: not anywhere.
All that was left was a mountain of debt for the sake and food they had consumed. ‖ 飲み食い: eating and drinking. 山のような: like a mountain. 借金: debt.
A dream…? The money I found was a dream, but the money I spent is real? ‖ 〜で: ~, while. The contrast carries the bitterness.
For a while, Katsugorō couldn't say a word. ‖ しばらく: for a while. 言えません: can't say.
I'm a pathetic man. I'd sunk so low I had a dream like that. ‖ 情けねえ = 情けない: pathetic, shameful. 〜ほど: to the point of ~.
All right, I've decided. From today, I quit drinking. Not a single drop. ‖ 一滴も〜ない: not a single drop. 飲まねえ = 飲まない.
Really, dear? ‖ 〜かい: asking back (old casual).
Yes, really. From tomorrow, I'll work honestly. ‖ まじめに: seriously, diligently. 働く: work.
His wife said nothing and quietly wiped away her tears. ‖ 〜ずに: without ~. そっと: quietly. 涙をふく: wipe tears.
True to his word, from the next day Katsugorō went to the market every morning while it was still dark. ‖ 〜どおり: just as ~. 〜うちに: while still ~. 〜ようになりました: came to ~ (change in habit).
Could Katsugorō really change? The answer comes in the next story. ‖ 変われる: be able to change. 〜でしょうか: I wonder if ~.
