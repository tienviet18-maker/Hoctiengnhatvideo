Long ago, at a small temple on top of a mountain, there was a head priest who thought he knew a great deal. ‖ 物知り: knowledgeable person. 〜と思っている: think of oneself as ~. 和尚: head priest.
Even when there was something he didn't know, he would never say "I don't know." ‖ 〜ても: even if. 決して〜ない: never.
One day, the priest's stomach was upset, so he had the doctor come. ‖ 調子が悪い: feel unwell. 〜てもらう: have someone ~.
Priest, by the way, do you have any "tenshiki"? ‖ ところで: by the way. 〜かな: (old-fashioned question).
T-tenshiki? ‖ (flustered)
The priest had never heard that word before. ‖ 〜たことがない: have never ~.
But he thought it would be embarrassing to say he didn't know. ‖ はずかしい: embarrassing.
Yes, yes, tenshiki. As it happens, we have none at the moment. ‖ 〜のです: (explaining).
Oh, none? Then I'll prescribe some medicine. ‖ 〜ておきましょう: I'll ~ (in advance).
When the doctor left, the priest at once called his young apprentice, Chinnen. ‖ 小僧: young apprentice monk. 呼ぶ: call.
Chinnen. Go down to town and borrow some tenshiki. ‖ 〜てきなさい: go and ~ (and come back).
What's tenshiki? ‖ 〜って: (called) ~.
You don't even know that? You'll see when you get there. Hurry along. ‖ 〜も知らないのか: you don't even know ~? 〜ば分かる: if you ~, you'll understand.
Tilting his head in puzzlement, Chinnen went down the long stone steps toward the town at the foot of the mountain. ‖ 首をかしげる: tilt one's head. 石段: stone steps. ふもと: foot of a mountain.
First, he went to the flower shop to ask. ‖ 〜てみる: try ~.
Excuse me. Please lend me some tenshiki. ‖ 貸す: lend.
Tenshiki? Oh, that. Unfortunately, it all sold out last night. ‖ あいにく: unfortunately. 売れる: sell. 〜てしまう: (all) ended up ~.
Next, he tried the secondhand shop. ‖ 古道具屋: secondhand shop.
Tenshiki, eh? We had one too, but I just lent it to someone the other day. ‖ ついこの間: just the other day. 〜てしまって: went and ~.
After that came the tofu shop. ‖ 豆腐屋: tofu shop.
Tenshiki? That stuff's delicious in miso soup. But I'm all out today. ‖ みそ汁: miso soup. 切らす: run out of.
You put it in miso soup? ‖ 〜ものなんですか: is it something you ~?
W-well, let's just say some households do. ‖ 〜ってことさ: that's what it is.
Nobody in town would admit they didn't know, either. ‖ 〜とは言わない: won't say ~.
How strange. No matter who I ask, nobody has any. ‖ 〜ても: no matter ~.
At a loss, Chinnen went to the doctor's house to pick up the medicine. ‖ 困る: be at a loss.
Doctor, what on earth is tenshiki? ‖ いったい: on earth.
Tenshiki is a word that appears in old Chinese medical books. ‖ 医学: medicine (study). 出てくる: appear.
It means a fart. ‖ おなら: fart.
A-a fart? ‖ (surprised)
Chinnen nearly burst out laughing. ‖ 思わず: in spite of oneself. ふき出す: burst out laughing. 〜そうになる: nearly ~.
I see. The priest didn't know, but pretended he did. ‖ 〜くせに: even though. 〜ふりをする: pretend to ~.
On the way back, Chinnen thought up a little prank. ‖ いたずら: prank. 〜(よ)うと思いつく: come up with the idea of ~.
Ah, Chinnen. Were you able to borrow the tenshiki? ‖ 〜てこられた: were able to ~ and come back.
Priest, there wasn't any anywhere in town. ‖ どこにも〜ない: not anywhere.
Of course. It's a rare thing, after all. ‖ めったにない: rare.
By the way, Priest, you do know what tenshiki is, don't you? ‖ ご存じ: know (honorific).
O-of course. More to the point, did you find out? Tell me. ‖ 〜こそ: (you) for your part. 〜てみなさい: try ~.
Yes. They say tenshiki means a cup for drinking sake. ‖ 盃: sake cup. 〜そうです: I hear.
Yes, yes. A sake cup. Well understood. ‖ よく: well.
The priest believed it completely. ‖ 信じこむ: firmly believe. すっかり: completely.
A few days later, the doctor came again to see how the priest was doing. ‖ 数日: a few days. 様子を見る: check on.
Priest, how are you feeling? Did you find any tenshiki? ‖ 具合: condition. いかが: how.
Yes, Doctor. As a matter of fact, we did. ‖ 実は: actually.
Chinnen, bring that tenshiki here. ‖ 持ってくる: bring.
Holding back his laughter, Chinnen carried in a paulownia-wood box. ‖ こらえる: hold back. 桐: paulownia.
With great ceremony, the priest opened the lid. ‖ もったいぶる: make a show. ふた: lid.
This is our temple's pride: a tenshiki handed down for three hundred years. ‖ 伝わる: be handed down. 自慢: pride. 〜でございます: is (very polite).
Inside was a splendid old sake cup. ‖ 立派: fine, splendid.
Priest, this is a sake cup. ‖ (surprised)
Yes, it is a tenshiki. ‖ (proudly)
No, no — tenshiki means a fart. ‖ いやいや: no, no.
A-a fart? ‖ (stunned)
The priest's face turned bright red in an instant. ‖ みるみるうちに: before one's eyes. 真っ赤: bright red.
Chinnen. You tricked me! ‖ だます: trick.
It's because you pretended to know, Priest. ‖ 〜からですよ: it's because ~.
Still, a tenshiki handed down for three hundred years — that's quite old. ‖ それにしても: even so. 〜とは: (exclamation) to think ~.
Yes. It's so old that it no longer makes a sound or a smell. ‖ あまり〜ので: so ~ that. いたしません: does not (humble).
When you don't know something, say you don't know. That's the least embarrassing way. ‖ 方法: way, method.
Pretending to know may get you by in the moment, but later you'll end up far more embarrassed. ‖ 知ったかぶり: pretending to know. ごまかす: gloss over. はじをかく: be embarrassed.
When you hear a word you don't know, do you pretend to know it? ‖ 〜ていませんか: aren't you ~?
And that is the end of the story of Tenshiki. ‖ おしまい: the end.
