Long ago, in a row house in Edo, there lived a carefree young man named Yotarō. ‖ 長屋: Edo row house. のんき: easygoing, carefree. 若者: young person.
He did no work at all, and just slept every day from morning till night. ‖ 〜ないで: without ~ing. 〜てばかりいる: do nothing but ~.
His worried uncle called Yotarō over one day. ‖ 叔父: uncle. 呼ぶ: to call.
Yotarō, isn't it about time you tried earning some money yourself? ‖ そろそろ: about time. 稼ぐ: to earn. 〜てみたらどうだ: why not try ~.
Earning? What do I have to do, Uncle? ‖ 〜って: (casual) ~ means.
I run a business selling old things. Tonight, you try setting up the stall in my place. ‖ 商売: business. 代わりに: instead. 店を出す: to open a stall.
A junk shop? Sounds fun. I'll give it a try. ‖ 道具屋: secondhand goods dealer. 〜そう: looks ~.
The uncle wrapped an old saw, a hanging scroll, a sword, a flute and more in a big cloth. ‖ のこぎり: saw. 掛け軸: hanging scroll. 笛: flute. ふろしき: wrapping cloth. 包む: to wrap.
Listen. The prices are written here. It's fine to knock a little off. ‖ まける: to give a discount. 〜ても構わない: it's fine to ~.
And in front of customers, you don't need to mention what's wrong with the goods. ‖ 品物: goods. 〜なくていい: don't have to ~. 〜ぞ: (male) you hear.
Yes, got it. I won't mention the bad points and just leave them hanging. ‖ 〜っぱなし: do ~ and leave it so (Yotarō misuses it).
Not "leave them hanging" — don't say them! Are you really going to be all right? ‖ 本当に: really.
And so Yotarō spread out a straw mat on a busy street corner and sat down. ‖ にぎやか: lively. 角: corner. ござ: straw mat. 広げる: to spread out.
After a while, a carpenter stopped. ‖ しばらくすると: after a while. 大工: carpenter.
Hey, young fellow. Let me take a look at that saw there. ‖ 兄さん: young man (address). 〜てくれ: ~ for me.
Go ahead. I hear this is a pretty good saw. ‖ なかなか: quite. 〜そうです: I hear.
Let's see. What's this? The blade is red with rust! ‖ 刃: blade. さびる: to rust. 〜じゃないか: isn't it?
Oh, my uncle picked that up after a fire. ‖ 火事: fire. 拾う: to pick up.
Picked up after a fire? A thing like that is bound to cut badly. ‖ 〜に決まっている: bound to ~. よく切れる: to cut well.
The carpenter walked off, disgusted. ‖ あきれる: to be fed up.
That's odd. I only told the truth. ‖ おかしい: strange. 〜だけなのに: I only ~, and yet.
Next came a refined old lady in a kimono. ‖ 上品: elegant, refined.
Excuse me, who painted the picture on that scroll? ‖ ちょいと: say (old-fashioned). どなた: who (polite). 描く: to paint.
My uncle said, "It's a fake of a famous master's painting." ‖ 有名: famous. にせ物: fake.
You sell a fake so openly? ‖ 堂々と: openly, boldly.
Yes. The real one's expensive, but the fake is cheap, so it's a bargain! ‖ 本物: the genuine article. お得: good deal.
The old lady went home laughing, without buying anything. ‖ 〜ずに: without ~ing.
Late in the night, this time a samurai came along. ‖ 更ける: (night) to grow late.
Townsman, show me that sword. ‖ 町人: townsman. 〜てみよ: show me (samurai command).
The samurai took the sword in his hand and tried to draw it from the scabbard. ‖ さや: scabbard. 抜く: to draw. 〜(よ)うとする: try to ~.
But no matter how hard he pulled, the sword wouldn't budge an inch. ‖ どんなに〜ても: no matter how. びくとも動かない: won't budge.
Hmm. Why won't this sword come out? ‖ 抜けぬ: = 抜けない (archaic). 〜のじゃ: (samurai speech).
Um, I think my uncle stuck it together with glue. ‖ たしか: I believe. 糊: glue. くっつける: to stick together.
With glue? A sword that can't be drawn is completely useless. ‖ 役に立たん: = 役に立たない, useless.
But sir, thanks to it not coming out, you don't have to worry about getting hurt. ‖ 〜おかげで: thanks to ~. けが: injury.
Fool! A sword exists for hurting people! ‖ ばかもの: fool. 〜ためにある: exists for ~.
The samurai went home angry. ‖ 怒る: to get angry.
Yotarō looked up at the night sky and sighed. ‖ 見上げる: to look up at. ため息をつく: to sigh.
This junk-selling business sure is hard. ‖ 〜っていうのは: this thing called ~. 〜なあ: sure is.
If I go home without selling a single thing, Uncle's going to scold me. ‖ 一つも〜ない: not even one. しかる: to scold.
Just then, a slightly drunk man came staggering along. ‖ 酔う: to get drunk. ふらふら: staggering.
Oh, there's a flute. I was good at the flute when I was young, you know. ‖ ほう: oh. 若いころ: in one's youth. 得意: good at.
The man picked up the flute and tried putting one finger into a hole. ‖ 穴: hole. 〜本: counter for long things.
Then his finger got stuck in the hole and wouldn't come out. ‖ はまる: to get stuck in. 抜ける: to come out.
Ow, ow, ow! Hey, young fellow, my finger won't come out! ‖ いたたた: ouch.
That's terrible. Well then, you have no choice but to buy that flute, sir. ‖ 〜しかない: have no choice but to ~.
Can't be helped. How much? ‖ しかたがない: it can't be helped. 〜だい: (casual) ~?
Let's see. One ryō. ‖ 両: ryō (Edo gold coin).
One ryō? A minute ago, it said "twenty mon" right here! ‖ 文: mon (small Edo coin).
A minute ago, your finger wasn't in it. ‖ 〜から: because.
You rascal, you saw I was in trouble and raised the price! ‖ こいつ: this guy. 値段を上げる: to raise the price.
Yes. My uncle said, "Watch the customer carefully before you sell." ‖ 様子: state, appearance. 売れ: sell (command).
Grumbling all the while, the man paid and went home, flute and all. ‖ ぶつぶつ言う: to grumble. 〜ながらも: while still ~. 〜ごと: ~ and all.
The next morning, Yotarō told his uncle everything. ‖ 全部: everything.
The saw, the scroll and the sword — none of them sold? ‖ 〜も〜も: both ~ and ~.
No. Everyone went away as soon as they heard what I had to say. ‖ 〜と: as soon as.
Well, that's because you blabbed about every bad point. ‖ そりゃあ: well, naturally. しゃべる: to chatter.
Yes. But the flute sold for one ryō! ‖ 売れる: to sell.
What, one ryō? That cheap flute? ‖ なに: what.
Yes. It came with a free finger, you see. ‖ おまけ: free extra.
The uncle didn't know whether to be happy or angry. ‖ 〜ていいのか: whether one should ~.
Yotarō, whether you've got a talent for business or not, I can't tell at all. ‖ 才能: talent. さっぱり分からん: can't make head or tail of it.
