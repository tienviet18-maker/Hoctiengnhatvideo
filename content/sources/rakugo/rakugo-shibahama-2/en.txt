In the last story, Katsugorō the fishmonger decided to quit drinking. ‖ 前の: previous. 〜と決める: decide that ~.
From then on, through rain and wind, Katsugorō worked without taking a single day off. ‖ 〜も〜も: both ~ and ~. 一日も〜ずに: without ~ even one day.
Even when friends offered him sake, Katsugorō shook his head. ‖ すすめられる: be offered (passive of すすめる). 〜ても: even if ~. 首を横にふる: shake one's head (refuse).
Sorry. I've quit drinking. ‖ すまねえ = すまない: sorry (Edo speech). 〜んだ: explaining.
He'd always had a sharp eye for fish. Word soon spread that Katsugorō's fish were fresh and tasty. ‖ もともと: originally. 確か: reliable, sharp. 評判になる: become well spoken of.
One regular customer, then two, and little by little he began to pay off his debts. ‖ お得意さん: regular customer. 〜ようになりました: became able to ~.
After one year, the debts were completely gone. ‖ 〜と: when ~. すっかり: completely.
After two years, the business had grown enough to hire young workers. ‖ 雇える: can hire (potential). 〜ほど: enough to ~.
His wife came to the shop every day too, keeping the accounts and helping out. ‖ 帳面をつける: keep the books. 手伝う: help.
And in the third year, he was able to open his own shop on the main street, small as it was. ‖ 表通り: main street. 〜ながらも: although ~. 〜ことができた: was able to ~.
This is the story of New Year's Eve that year. ‖ 大みそか: New Year's Eve. 〜のことです: it is about ~.
Outside, the temple bells of New Year's Eve were ringing. ‖ 除夜の鐘: New Year's Eve bells. 鳴る: ring.
The joya bell is rung one hundred and eight times at temples on the last night of the year. ‖ つく: strike (a bell). The 108 strokes are said to drive away 108 earthly desires.
What a nice sound. Another year ended safely. ‖ 〜だなあ: exclamation. 無事に: safely, without trouble.
It really did. You worked so hard. ‖ よくがんばったね: you really worked hard.
It's all thanks to you. Me three years ago, I was awful. ‖ 〜のおかげ: thanks to ~. ひどい: terrible. 〜からな: since ~ (male, casual).
Dear, today there's something I want you to hear. ‖ 〜てほしい: want (someone) to ~. 〜の: soft feminine ending.
His wife brought an old leather wallet from the back room. ‖ 奥: the back of the house. 〜てきました: brought ~ out.
…This wallet. I feel like I've seen it somewhere. ‖ 〜たことがある: have ~ed before. 〜ような: seems like (trailing off).
It's the wallet you picked up on Shiba beach three years ago. ‖ 〜てきた: picked up and brought home. あの: that ~ (we both know).
What? So that wasn't a dream! ‖ なんだって: what? 〜じゃなかったのか: so it wasn't ~?
Katsugorō couldn't believe his eyes. ‖ 信じられない: can't believe. 自分の目: one's own eyes.
I'm sorry. When I said it was a dream, that was a lie. ‖ ごめんなさい: I'm sorry. うそ: a lie.
His wife slowly began telling the story of three years before. ‖ ゆっくりと: slowly. 始める: begin.
That day, while you were asleep, I went to the landlord for advice. ‖ 〜間に: while ~. 大家さん: landlord (in Edo, also a neighborhood overseer). 相談に行く: go for advice.
The landlord said: if you spend money you found, it's a crime. In the old days it was a crime heavy enough to cost your head. ‖ 〜たら: if ~. 罪になる: become a crime. 首が飛ぶ: be beheaded. 〜って: quoting.
In the Edo period, keeping found money for yourself was a very serious crime. ‖ 落とし物: lost property. 自分のものにする: make it one's own.
If they'd been found out, they could have lost both the shop and the house. ‖ 見つかれば: if found out. 〜ところだった: nearly ~.
So the landlord and I took the money to the magistrate's office. ‖ お役所: government office. 届ける: hand in, report.
We decided between us that, for you, it would be a dream. ‖ 〜ことにしよう: let's treat it as ~. 〜って: quoting.
A year passed and no owner came forward, so the money came back to us as the finder's. ‖ 〜ても: even after ~. 落とし主: the person who lost it. 〜として: as ~.
But I was afraid that if I showed you right away, you'd drown in sake again… so I kept quiet until today. ‖ 〜たら: if ~. 酒におぼれる: drown in drink. 黙る: keep quiet.
I'm truly sorry for lying to you. If you're angry, you can hit me. ‖ うそをつく: tell a lie. 〜なら: if ~. 〜てもいい: it's okay to ~.
Katsugorō stared at the wallet in silence for a while. ‖ 黙って: in silence. 見つめる: stare at.
What are you saying? Lift your head. ‖ 頭を上げる: raise one's head (stop bowing in apology). 〜てくれ: please ~.
I'm the one who should apologize. ‖ 謝らなきゃ = 謝らなければ. 〜のほう: the one, the side.
If I had spent this money back then, I'd be in jail right now. ‖ もし〜ていたら: if I had ~. 今ごろ: about now. 牢屋: jail.
I'd have drowned in drink, ruined my health, and made your life hard too. ‖ 体をこわす: ruin one's health. 苦労をかける: cause someone hardship. 〜ただろう: would probably have ~.
Because you lied for me, I was able to become a decent man. Thank you. ‖ 〜てくれたから: because you ~ for me. まともな: decent. 〜になれた: was able to become ~.
Dear… ‖ Trailing off, overcome with feeling.
His wife wiped her tears and smiled. ‖ 涙をふく: wipe tears. にっこり: smile.
It's New Year's Eve, after all. I've got sake ready. Drink three years' worth. ‖ 〜だもの: after all (feminine, coaxing). 用意してある: has been prepared. 〜てちょうだい: please ~ (feminine).
Is it really all right? …My first sake in three years. ‖ 〜ぶり: for the first time in ~. 〜かい: asking back.
His wife filled a teacup to the brim with sake. ‖ 湯のみ: teacup. なみなみと: to the brim. つぐ: pour.
The lovely smell of sake filled the room. ‖ 香り: aroma. 広がる: spread.
Katsugorō brought the cup close to his mouth and smelled it. ‖ 〜まで持っていく: bring up to ~. においをかぐ: smell.
Ah, that smells good. ‖ 〜だ: assertive, exclamatory (male).
But before drinking, Katsugorō put the cup down. ‖ ところが: but (unexpectedly). 〜前に: before ~. 置く: put down.
What's wrong? Aren't you going to drink? ‖ どうしたの: what's wrong. 〜のかい: asking (old style).
No, I'll leave it. ‖ やめておこう: I'll pass (〜ておく: decide in advance).
It'd be bad if it turned into a dream again. ‖ 〜と、いけねえ = 〜と、いけない: it would be bad if ~. The famous punchline: he fears his happiness might turn out to be a dream.
His wife laughed happily. ‖ うれしそうに: happily (〜そう: looking ~).
That is the punchline of "Shibahama", a masterpiece of ninjō-banashi, rakugo about human feeling. ‖ 人情噺: moving rakugo about human kindness. 名作: masterpiece.
Many rakugo fans look forward to hearing this story on New Year's Eve. ‖ 楽しみにする: look forward to. 〜のを: the act of ~.
