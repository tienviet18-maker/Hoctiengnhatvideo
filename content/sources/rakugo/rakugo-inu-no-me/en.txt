Long ago, in the town of Edo, there was a carpenter called Gen. ‖ 大工: carpenter. 〜という: called ~.
For some time now, Gen had been troubled because his eyes were in very bad shape. ‖ ここしばらく: for a while now. 調子が悪い: in bad shape. 困る: to be troubled.
When I get up in the morning, my eyes are blurry, and I can only see everything vaguely. ‖ かすむ: to blur. 何もかも: everything. ぼんやり: dimly.
Unable to go to work, the troubled Gen visited a doctor who was well known in town. ‖ 〜ず: without/unable (= ないで). 評判: reputation. たずねる: to visit.
This doctor was skilled, but famous for doing things in a rather unusual way. ‖ 腕がいい: skilled. 変わった: unusual. 〜ことで有名: famous for ~.
Welcome. What seems to be the trouble today? ‖ いらっしゃい: welcome. どうなさいました: (respectful) what's wrong.
Doctor, I can't see well, so I can't do my work. ‖ 仕事にならない: can't get any work done.
Well, let me take a look. ‖ どれ: well, let's see. 〜てごらんなさい: try ~.
The doctor peered closely into Gen's eyes. ‖ じっと: intently. のぞきこむ: to peer into.
Aha, this is bad. Your eyeballs are completely dirty. ‖ ははあ: aha. 目の玉: eyeball. 汚れる: to get dirty. 〜な: (older speech) ~, I see.
Dirty? Then what should I do? ‖ 〜って: ~? (echoing). 〜ばいい: should just ~.
It's simple. We take your eyeballs out and give them a good wash. ‖ 簡単: simple. 取り出す: to take out. 洗う: to wash.
T-take them out? Doctor, is that really safe? ‖ 大丈夫: all right.
Don't worry. I've already done it for hundreds of people. ‖ 心配いらない: no need to worry. 何百人: hundreds of people.
The doctor brought a small spoon and, pop, pop, took out both eyeballs. ‖ 匙: spoon. すぽん: pop (coming out).
Whoa, it's pitch dark. I can't see a thing. ‖ 真っ暗: pitch dark.
Of course. Your eyeballs are right here now. ‖ 当たり前: natural, of course.
The doctor carefully washed the eyeballs in medicinal water. ‖ 薬: medicine. ていねいに: carefully.
Right, they're clean. While I'm at it, let's dry them a little. ‖ ついでに: while at it. 乾かす: to dry. 〜ておく: do ~ in advance.
With that, the doctor laid the eyeballs out on a plate on the veranda. ‖ 縁側: veranda. 皿: plate. 並べる: to line up.
The soft autumn sunlight made the two eyeballs sparkle. ‖ やわらかい: soft. 照らす: to shine on. きらきら: sparkling.
They'll take a while to dry, so please lie down. ‖ 乾く: to dry. 横になる: to lie down.
While the doctor was drinking tea inside, a neighbor's dog came into the garden. ‖ 奥: inner part of the house. 〜あいだに: while ~. 近所: neighborhood.
When the dog found the round things on the plate, it sniffed at them. ‖ 丸い: round. くんくん: sniff sniff. においをかぐ: to smell.
And, perhaps thinking they looked tasty, it gobbled up both of them. ‖ 〜と思ったのか: perhaps thinking ~. 二つとも: both. ぺろりと: in one gulp.
A little later, the apprentice came out to the veranda and shouted. ‖ 弟子: apprentice. 大声を上げる: to shout.
Doctor, it's terrible! The eyeballs on the plate are gone! ‖ 大変: terrible.
What, gone? That can't be. Look carefully. ‖ 〜はずはない: can't be ~.
I looked, but they're nowhere. Oh! That dog's mouth is all wet. ‖ ぬれる: to get wet.
Oh no. The dog ate them, did it? ‖ しまった: oh no.
Even the doctor turned pale. ‖ さすがに: even (someone like). 青くなる: to turn pale.
I can't send a patient home without his eyeballs. ‖ 〜まま: still in that state. 〜わけにはいかない: can't very well ~.
But Doctor, there's no way to get them back now. ‖ 取り返す: to get back. 〜ようがない: there's no way to ~.
No, there is a way. Go and catch that dog. ‖ 方法: method. つかまえる: to catch.
What are you going to do with the dog? ‖ どうする: what to do.
If it ate the eyeballs, we just put its eyes into the patient instead. ‖ 〜のなら: if (it's the case that) ~. 代わりに: instead.
What? You're putting dog's eyes into a person? ‖ ええっ: what?!
There's no time for complaining. Hurry up. ‖ 文句: complaint.
And so the doctor took out the dog's eyeballs and put them into Gen's face. ‖ こうして: and so.
Now, slowly try opening your eyes. ‖ ゆっくり: slowly. 開ける: to open.
The moment Gen opened his eyes, dazzling light came flooding in. ‖ 〜たとたん: the moment ~. まぶしい: dazzling. 飛びこむ: to rush in.
Oh, I can see! I can see! Much more clearly than before! ‖ はっきり: clearly. ずっと: much (more).
That's good. How is it? Anything strange? ‖ 変な: strange.
No, it's as if I've been born again. Thank you, Doctor. ‖ まるで〜みたい: just like ~. 生まれ変わる: to be reborn.
Gen thanked him again and again and went home in high spirits. ‖ 何度も: many times. 元気に: cheerfully.
On the way home, Gen was so happy that he kept looking up at the sky and the rooftops. ‖ 帰り道: way home. 屋根: roof. 見上げる: to look up at.
From then on, Gen could see very well. ‖ それからというもの: ever since then. 〜ようになる: come to ~.
Even on a dark road at night, he could see far ahead, just like in the daytime. ‖ 同じように: in the same way. 遠く: far away.
Even if there was a faint sound far away, he noticed it right away. ‖ かすか: faint. 気がつく: to notice.
However, about a month later, Gen came to the doctor's place again. ‖ 〜ほど: about. たつ: (time) to pass.
Oh, Gen. How are your eyes? ‖ おや: oh. 具合: condition.
My eyes see really well. But there's something a bit troubling. ‖ 〜まして: (polite) ~, and so.
Something troubling? Tell me. ‖ 〜てごらんなさい: go ahead and ~.
Whenever I smell meat or fish, for some reason I start drooling. ‖ なぜか: for some reason. よだれ: drool.
And when a stranger walks past my house, I just feel like growling. ‖ それに: besides. うなる: to growl.
Hmm, you're getting a little dog-like, then. ‖ ほう: hmm. 〜らしい: -like.
There's more. This is what troubles me most. ‖ 一番: most.
What is it? ‖ なん: what.
Whenever I see a tree by the road, I can't help wanting to lift one leg. ‖ 道ばた: roadside. どうしても: no matter what. 片方: one side.
Lift one leg, you say? ‖ 〜ですか: ~, you say?
Yes. Just yesterday, in the middle of work, I saw a pillar in a house and almost lifted my leg. ‖ 途中: in the middle of. 柱: pillar. もう少しで〜ところだった: almost ~.
The doctor spat out his tea in spite of himself and couldn't say anything for a while. ‖ 思わず: in spite of oneself. ふき出す: to spit out, burst out laughing.
