January 25th is the first Tenjin festival day of the year. It is called "Hatsu Tenjin". ‖ 初めての: first. 〜と言います: is called ~. Tenjin: the shrine deity Sugawara no Michizane.
Tenjin-sama is the god of study. On festival days, lots of stalls set up in front of the shrine. ‖ 勉強の神様: god of learning (students pray to pass exams). 店が出る: stalls appear.
Kuma-san was about to go and pray at the Tenjin shrine by himself. ‖ 〜ようとしていました: was about to ~. お参り: a visit to pray at a shrine/temple.
Hey, if you're going out, take Kinbō with you. ‖ あんた: "you" (wife to husband, casual). 〜なら: if ~. 連れていく: take along.
No way. If I take him, it's "buy me that, buy me this" — he's a pain. ‖ 〜と: whenever ~. あれ買って、これ買って: buy me that, buy me this. うるさい: noisy, annoying.
Just then, his son Kinbō came running. ‖ そこへ: just then. 走ってくる: come running.
Dad, where are you going? Tenjin-sama? I'm coming too! ‖ おとっつぁん: "Dad" (Edo-period). おいら: "me" (boys, casual).
No. You always ask me to buy you something. ‖ だめだ: no. すぐ: at the drop of a hat. 〜から: because.
I won't. Today I won't ask for anything. I promise. ‖ 何も〜ない: not ~ anything. 約束する: promise.
Really? If you ask even once, we're going straight home. ‖ 〜でも: even ~. 〜たら: if ~. 〜ぞ: emphasis (male).
Okay. I'll be good the whole time. ‖ いい子にする: be good. 〜てる = 〜ている.
And so the two of them headed to the shrine hand in hand. ‖ こうして: and so. 手をつなぐ: hold hands. 〜へ向かう: head for ~.
Both sides of the road were lined with candy, dango and toy stalls. ‖ 両側: both sides. 〜や〜や: ~, ~ and so on. 並ぶ: be lined up.
Dad, I'm being good today, right? ‖ 〜だろう: right? (seeking agreement).
Yeah, you're good. ‖ ああ: yeah (male).
Since I'm good, will you buy me something as a reward? ‖ ごほうび: reward. 〜てくれる: (someone) does ~ for me.
…There it is. That's why I didn't want to bring you. ‖ ほら: see, there. だから: that's why. 〜たくなかった: didn't want to ~.
Kinbō stopped in front of the candy stall and wouldn't budge. ‖ 止まる: stop. 動かなくなる: stop moving.
Candy! Just one candy! Just one is fine! ‖ 〜だけ: only ~. 〜でいい: ~ is fine.
Kinbō looked like he was about to cry loudly, so the people around were looking their way. ‖ 泣きそうになる: be about to cry. 〜ので: so. 周り: surroundings.
All right, all right. Just one. Hey, candy man, give me one. ‖ わかった: all right. 〜くれ: give me (male, blunt).
Yes sir, which one? There are red ones and blue ones too. ‖ へい: yes (Edo merchant). どれにしますか: which will you have?
The smallest one will do. ‖ 一番: most. 小さいの: the small one (の replaces a noun).
But then Kuma-san started choosing the candy himself. ‖ ところが: but then. 〜始める: start ~.
This one's too small. This one's a bad colour. …Which one's good? ‖ 〜すぎる: too ~. 色が悪い: bad colour. 〜かな: I wonder.
Sir, please don't touch them with your fingers so much. ‖ 〜ないでください: please don't ~. そんなに: that much.
If I don't choose properly, it's unfair to the kid. ‖ ちゃんと: properly. 〜ないと: if not ~. かわいそう: poor thing.
Having finally got his candy, Kinbō walked along licking it happily. The next place he stopped was in front of the dango stall. ‖ 〜てもらう: have (someone) do for you. うれしそうに: happily. 〜ながら: while ~. 次に: next. 〜のは〜でした: the place ~ was ~.
Dad, dango! I want to eat that mitarashi dango! ‖ みたらし団子: dango with sweet-salty sauce. 〜たい: want to ~.
I bought you candy a moment ago. ‖ さっき: a moment ago. 〜だろう: didn't I?
The candy's all gone. ‖ なくなっちゃった = なくなってしまった: it's all gone.
That was fast. …Oh well. Dango man, one stick of mitarashi. ‖ しょうがない: it can't be helped. 一本: one (counter for long things).
Mitarashi dango are dumplings covered in plenty of sweet and salty sauce. ‖ 甘くてしょっぱい: sweet and salty. たっぷり: plenty.
Before handing the dango to his son, Kuma-san licked off all the sauce. ‖ 〜前に: before ~. ぺろぺろ: lick-lick. 〜てしまいました: ended up ~.
Ah, Dad, the sauce is all gone! ‖ あっ: ah! 〜よ: emphasis.
Quiet. You just do this. ‖ こうすれば: if you do this. 〜んだ: explanation.
Kuma-san dipped the licked dango into the sauce pot once more. ‖ もう一度: once more. つぼ: pot, jar.
Hey, sir! Please don't put things you've licked in there! ‖ ちょっと: hey (protest). 〜ないでください: please don't ~.
I only dipped it once. …Oh, tasty. One more time. ‖ 〜ただけ: only ~. うまい: tasty (male).
I said no! That pot can't be used any more! ‖ 〜って: I said (repeating, annoyed). 使えません: can't be used.
The dango man got angry, picked up the pot and went off into the back of the stall. Even so, there was still something Kinbō wanted. ‖ 怒る: get angry. 奥へ入る: go into the back. それでも: even so. 欲しい: want (a thing).
Dad, that kite! I want that big kite! ‖ 凧: kite (flown at New Year in Japan).
A kite? Come on, that's enough. ‖ いいかげんにしろ: that's enough (scolding).
If you buy me the kite, I won't ask for anything else. It really is the last thing. ‖ 〜てくれたら: if you ~ for me. 最後: last.
In the end, Kuma-san went and bought the biggest kite. ‖ 結局: in the end. 〜てしまいました: ended up ~.
Here you are. There's wind today, so it'll fly well. ‖ 風がある: it's windy. 上がる: go up.
The two went to the big field behind the shrine. ‖ 裏: behind. 野原: field, meadow.
Right, Dad will fly it for you. Watch closely. ‖ 〜てやる: do ~ for (someone below). 見ていろ: watch (command).
When Kuma-san ran, the kite caught the wind and rose higher and higher. ‖ 〜と: when ~. 風に乗る: ride the wind. 高く高く: higher and higher.
Oh, it's up, it's up! That feels great! ‖ 気持ちがいい: feels good. 〜なあ: exclamation.
Dad, let me hold it too. ‖ 持たせる: let (someone) hold (causative).
Wait, wait. It's just getting good. ‖ 待て: wait (command). いいところ: the good part.
Kuma-san was completely absorbed, as if he'd gone back to being a child. ‖ 〜のように: as if ~. 夢中になる: become absorbed.
Hey, it's my kite. Lend it to me just for a bit. ‖ 〜てよ: pleading. 貸す: lend.
Be quiet! Kids, go over there! ‖ あっち: over there. 〜てろ = 〜ていろ: command.
Kinbō puffed out his cheeks and said: ‖ ほっぺた: cheeks. ふくらませる: puff up.
If I'd known this would happen, I'd never have brought Dad! ‖ こんなことなら: if it was going to be like this. 〜なんか: the likes of ~ (dismissive). 〜んじゃなかった: I shouldn't have ~.
The father who didn't want to bring his child ends up hearing the very same words from him. This is the story's "ochi", the punchline. ‖ 言われてしまう: end up being told (passive + しまう). おち: rakugo punchline.
Perhaps parents and children on festival days haven't changed much, then or now. ‖ 昔も今も: then and now. 〜かもしれません: perhaps.
The next story is about a man who learns how to praise people but says nothing but odd things. ‖ ほめ方: how to praise. 〜ばかり: nothing but ~.
